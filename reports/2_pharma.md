## Part A. How drug companies sell when advertising is banned

### 1. The logo held on screen for an extra second (Mankind)
[11:14](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=674) · heard it
- India doesn't allow prescription drugs to be advertised to the public.
- So Mankind launched consumer (over-the-counter) products and ran ads for those, keeping the Mankind logo on screen for more than a second instead of the usual half-second.
- The aim was to make the company name familiar across India before its medicines reached each region.
- **Research:** seeing a name repeatedly makes people like it more, the mere-exposure effect (Zajonc 1968, paper, not re-read).

### 2. 17,000 sales reps
[43:44](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=2624) · heard it
- A guest: out of every ₹100 a drug sells for, it costs roughly ₹25–35 to make. Much of the rest pays for a sales force of about 17,000 medical reps who visit doctors.
- **Your takeaway:** in India, pharma marketing happens person to person, aimed at doctors rather than patients.

### 3. "Our industry is ethical"
[2:07:55](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=7675) · heard it
- A guest says the top 10 Indian pharma companies "don't do anything wrong".
- **Counterpoint:** in the 2022 Dolo-650 case, tax officials alleged that the maker spent around ₹1,000 crore on freebies for doctors. The company denied it (verify).
- India introduced a marketing code for pharma companies (UCPMP) in 2024 (verify).
- **Research:** gifts create a feeling of owing something back, reciprocity (Cialdini 1984). Studies link pharma payments to doctors with more prescribing of the paying company's drugs (DeJong et al. 2016, *JAMA Internal Medicine*, paper, not re-read).

### 4. "Every superhit drug gets abused"
[2:00:32](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=7232) · heard it
- On GLP-1 weight-loss drugs (Ozempic type): "whenever a product becomes superhit, it is being abused", and patients pressure doctors into prescribing them.
- At [2:20:03](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=8403), Nikhil asks whether a 25-year-old could build a GLP-1 brand the way people build protein brands. The answer: no, because it's prescription-only and can't be advertised.

### 5. "National security" as a sales line
[1:45:48](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=6348) · heard it
- One guest dismisses the idea that China controlling India's drug ingredients is a security risk: "a narrative I don't believe in".
- He adds that during COVID, Chinese suppliers were *more* reliable than Indian ones.
- **Your takeaway:** industries borrow security language to win protection. Ask who benefits when you hear it.

## Part B. Trump and pharma

### 6. Tariffs on drugs
[59:10](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=3550) · heard it
- Generic drugs aren't tariffed.
- Branded, patented drugs sold into the US but made elsewhere may face a 100% tariff.
- That hits India's contract drug-manufacturing (CDMO) firms.
- The US BIOSECURE Act pushes companies away from Chinese suppliers ("China Plus One"), which helps India ([2:32:30](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=9150)).

### 7. Cuts to research funding
[1:08:49](https://youtu.be/WMRO9dvD5T0?t=4129) · heard it
- A guest says Trump has cut a lot of public research funding.
- He quotes the National Cancer Institute at $40B a year. That looks wrong: it's closer to the whole NIH budget, and the NCI's share is a few billion (verify).

### 8. The Pfizer deal and TrumpRx (from your earlier session)
Verify all of these:
- **30 Sept 2025:** Trump announced a Pfizer deal with some price cuts and a "TrumpRx" website.
- **What Pfizer got:** a three-year break from planned tariffs.
- **The criticism:** a health-policy professor called selling straight to patients a gimmick that helps few people.
- **Ad crackdown:** in Sept 2025 there was an order to crack down on misleading drug ads on TV. The US and New Zealand are the only countries that allow prescription-drug ads aimed at the public.

## Part C. Selling hope: longevity and health gadgets

### 9. 100 pills a day and a website (Bryan Johnson, WTF Longevity)
[1:20:30–1:24:06](https://youtu.be/fEUoJSTYtyc?t=4830) · heard it
- **His regimen:** he takes about 100 pills a day (now mixed into a drink) and sends people to his Blueprint website for the list.
- **The pushback, from a doctor on the same panel:**
  - Take supplements only for deficiencies you can actually measure ([32:04](https://youtu.be/fEUoJSTYtyc?t=1924)).
  - Mitochondrial ones like NAD precursors "don't have any proof".
  - At [38:46](https://youtu.be/fEUoJSTYtyc?t=2326): "the entire supplement industry is based on 'take it, it won't hurt'".
- **Attention stunt:** at [30:31](https://youtu.be/fEUoJSTYtyc?t=1831), Johnson says a prediction market is launching a bet on his nighttime erections, and calls it a sign the idea "broke through the zeitgeist".
- **Research:**
  - **Supplements:** for most vitamins, the evidence is insufficient to say they prevent disease (US Preventive Services Task Force 2022, paper, not re-read).
  - **Expectation:** believing something works can change how you feel (placebo research).
  - **Authority:** the person who experiments on himself seems believable because he "has skin in the game".

### 10. "Money warps every relationship" (Johnson)
[23:49](https://youtu.be/fEUoJSTYtyc?t=1429) · heard it
- After selling his company for about $300M, Johnson says every relationship became "you, your money and the person".

### 11. The WHOOP band and the burger
[2:35:57](https://youtu.be/lRjprPQHuXw?t=9357) · heard it
- A guest describes someone wearing a WHOOP fitness band while eating a McDonald's burger and a cola: "just visual cues to tell people that I belong to this universe".
- **Research:** buying something to show which group you belong to is conspicuous consumption (Veblen 1899).

### 12. "If sleep was a drug…" (Brian Armstrong)
[2:03:51](https://youtu.be/p8O6SFwXqok?t=7431) · heard it

## Part D. Health claims from Predictive History (flagged)
- **COVID vaccines:**
  - Jiang uses vaccine mandates as his example of "reason replaced by rules" ([24:34](https://youtu.be/Ipj_Tdzokzo?t=1474)).
  - At [2:40:21](https://youtu.be/Z1G6WI1Q0M8?t=9621) he says the vaccines were "not tested" and "rushed", and that Sweden handled COVID best.
  - **Fact check:** the main COVID vaccines went through large trials before approval. Pfizer's had about 43,000 participants (Polack et al. 2020, *NEJM*). Sweden's results are still debated.
- **The Afghan opium claim:**
  - Jiang links the US occupation of Afghanistan to the Sackler family's opioids ([2:31:34](https://youtu.be/5rDMq-5_xkM?t=9094)).
  - **Fact check:** Afghan opium production did rise during the occupation (UN drug-office data). But Afghan heroin and Purdue's prescription OxyContin are separate stories, and the link between them is his.

## Part E. New in version 2 (Predictive History)

### 1. MK-Ultra: a real case of mind-control research
[44:00](https://youtu.be/6PdfD44DkqA?t=2640) · heard it
- Jiang describes the CIA's MK-Ultra programme: LSD and "brainwashing" experiments on people who didn't know, run by chemist Sidney Gottlieb, with most records destroyed.
- **This one is real history.** MK-Ultra ran from about 1953 to 1973. In 1973 the CIA ordered most of its files destroyed. The surviving papers came out through the 1975 Church Committee and a 1977 Senate hearing (verify exact dates).
- **The "oh" moment:** real abuses like this are why later, false conspiracy theories feel believable.

### 2. "I've never met anyone helped by a psychologist"
[47:00](https://youtu.be/6PdfD44DkqA?t=2820) · heard it
- This is one man's anecdote.
- **Research:** meta-analyses find psychotherapy has a sizeable average effect (Smith & Glass 1977, *American Psychologist*; Wampold & Imel, *The Great Psychotherapy Debate*, 2015; paper and book, not re-read).
- A good example of personal experience being used to dismiss a whole field.

### 3. The opium trade as a model
[26:00](https://youtu.be/CbamEcNuDXo?t=1560) and [32:00](https://youtu.be/CbamEcNuDXo?t=1920) · heard it
- Jiang: Britain sold opium to China with the help of local elites, and "the game is still being played today".
- **Real history:** the Opium Wars (1839–42 and 1856–60), fought by Britain to keep the opium trade open.
- **His add-on:** that the same game runs today is his theory.

### 4. COVID vaccines and "science as religion" (⚠ flagged)
[4:46](https://youtu.be/zAer-Mqe7tQ?t=286) · heard it
- He calls science "the dominant religion" and uses COVID vaccines as his example.
- This repeats the vaccine scepticism flagged in Part D. Large trials and real-world data showed the vaccines cut severe illness and death (paper, not re-read).

## Part F. New in version 3

- **Semmelweis and the maternity clinics** ([44:00](https://youtu.be/ra2v9HkaHz8?t=2640), heard it): two maternity clinics in the same hospital had very different death rates. Real history: Ignaz Semmelweis (1847) showed doctors' unwashed hands were killing mothers, and colleagues rejected him. A classic story of experts resisting evidence that blames them. It's also why the "Semmelweis reflex" became a phrase.
- **"Drugs" as a game-theory trap** (version 2, Report 2, Part E, item 3) is his main pharma theme. Predictive History has almost no real pharma content, so for pharma rely on the WTF episodes.
