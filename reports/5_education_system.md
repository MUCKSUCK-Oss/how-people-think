## Part A. Meritocracy: fair race or gatekeeping?

### 1. "Meritocracy is failing society" (Jiang, who went to Yale on a full scholarship)
[2:34](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=154) · heard it
- Jiang calls himself a product of meritocracy and is "very thankful" for Yale.
- He still wouldn't want his three kids to go there ([12:23](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=743)).
- His argument ([1:30:09](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=5409)):
  - Meritocracy "is really about gatekeeping: who wins and who loses".
  - Whether you get into Yale or Harvard "was already destined from the moment you're born".
- **Research:** children from the top 1% of families are about twice as likely to attend Ivy-Plus colleges as middle-class children with the same test scores (Chetty, Deming & Friedman 2023, paper, not re-read).

### 2. The more unequal a country, the more it believes in meritocracy
WTF, Nikhil with Ruchir Sharma: [27:12](https://youtu.be/lTCzIDITaac?t=1632) · heard it
- Nikhil raises the "Great Gatsby curve": more inequality goes with *more* belief in meritocracy.
- Ruchir: capitalism doesn't give equal outcomes, but it needs a *feeling* of equal opportunity.
- **Research:**
  - Mijs 2021, *Socio-Economic Review*: "The paradox of inequality: income inequality and belief in meritocracy go hand in hand".
  - Corak 2013, on the Great Gatsby curve.
  - Both papers, not re-read.
- **The "oh" moment:** the belief that the race is fair may be what makes an unfair race feel acceptable.

### 3. How the SAT made the Ivy League dominant (real history)
Jiang: [21:39](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=1299) and [32:58](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=1978) · heard it
- A hundred years ago the US had three kinds of universities:
  - the Ivy League, "a social club for the rich";
  - research universities for scholars;
  - state colleges that trained farmers and engineers.
- In the 1930s, Harvard president James B. Conant asked Henry Chauncey to build a national test (the SAT) to find "the best and brightest".
- **Research:** this history is documented in Nicholas Lemann, *The Big Test* (1999, book, not re-read). That's a good old book for you to read.
- **⚠ His add-on:** Jiang ties meritocracy to a secret Freemason "religion of reason" ([1:14:44](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=4484)). That part is his theory, with no evidence.

### 4. The death of childhood
Jiang: [19:05](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=1145) · heard it
- In Hong Kong, kindergartners drill vocabulary to get into a good primary school, then a good high school, then a good university.
- At McGill, students are burned out, raised by "helicopter parents", with "no freedom to fail".
- **Research:** the claim that social media and pressure are harming teen mental health is contested.
  - Jonathan Haidt and Jean Twenge argue the harm is big.
  - Orben & Przybylski (2019, *Nature Human Behaviour*) found small average effects.
  - Present both sides (papers, not re-read).

## Part B. What schools are "really" for (Jiang's harder claims; read critically)

- **"School makes you stupid":** "the purpose of school is not education, it is indoctrination" ([1:29:09](https://youtu.be/N2GFDDZtGQY?t=5349)).
  - He claims curious kids get sent to psychiatrists and put on Ritalin.
  - **Research:** the debate over ADHD over-diagnosis is real. For example, the youngest children in a class are more likely to be diagnosed (Morrow et al. 2012, *CMAJ*, paper, not re-read). But "teachers drug curious kids" is his exaggeration.
- **"Don't send your kids to school"** ([2:21:31](https://youtu.be/YyUl1QerHjA?t=8491)):
  - He calls school "the main vector to force compliance", including vaccine mandates and "turning kids against parents".
  - He homeschools his children and says "I don't teach them anything" ([2:23:37](https://youtu.be/YyUl1QerHjA?t=8617)).
- **"Denying children God is a lobotomy"** ([1:32:08](https://youtu.be/Ipj_Tdzokzo?t=5528)). He calls the school system "designed to be evil".
- **English-medium schooling = indoctrination** into a "materialistic, individualistic, liberal" worldview ([2:12:09](https://youtu.be/Ipj_Tdzokzo?t=7929)). That's an interesting point for India.
- **His ideal school** is a farm where kids learn as apprentices next to working adults ([1:23:21](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=5001)).
- **⚠ The big "oh, he's doing this" moment** ([1:18:44](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=4724)):
  - He chose to teach in China *because* it was "dysfunctional", so nobody would stop him teaching teenagers about "secret societies, eschatology and Kabbalah".
  - In his words, "the kids… believe whatever you say".
  - A teacher choosing a place where nobody checks him is exactly the gatekeeping problem in reverse.
- **One good idea from him** ([2:13:30](https://youtu.be/5rDMq-5_xkM?t=8010)): a teacher should say "I am wrong" every day, to model humility for students.

## Part E. New in version 2 (Predictive History)

### 1. "Why Schools Suck": the school as a game
[22:00](https://youtu.be/kS-muAuq62E?t=1320) to [37:00](https://youtu.be/kS-muAuq62E?t=2220) · heard it
- Jiang treats a school as a game with three players: parents, teachers and the school. The government "doesn't really care".
- **Parents** want "face": a child at an Ivy League school.
- **International schools** market themselves with foreign teachers' faces, not with evidence that kids learn.
- **The school** gives scholarships to a few top students to boost its results. Meanwhile there's "lots of cheating" and high turnover.
- **Research:** Campbell's law: the more a number (like admissions results) is used to judge something, the more it gets gamed (Campbell 1979, paper, not re-read).

### 2. The degree as a ticket
[22:00](https://youtu.be/QwfB-vXXKWU?t=1320) · heard it
- Jiang: students pay for "the degree… your ticket to success". The school doesn't "actually have to teach you anything".
- **Research:**
  - Signalling theory: a degree pays partly because it *signals* ability, not only because of what you learn (Spence 1973, paper, not re-read).
  - Bryan Caplan, *The Case Against Education* (2018, book, not re-read).

### 3. Harvard's origin and the SAT, again
[4:00](https://youtu.be/JU_8fJjtGxA?t=240), [8:00](https://youtu.be/JU_8fJjtGxA?t=480) and [12:00](https://youtu.be/JU_8fJjtGxA?t=720) · heard it
- Harvard was founded (1636) to train ministers. That's true.
- The SAT began as a scholarship test. That matches Lemann's *The Big Test*.
- To Harvard, "best" means "most likely to succeed", not most academic.

### 4. "University is a complete rip-off"
[1:01:00](https://youtu.be/hk_yhi3-prw?t=3660) · heard it
- He tells students heading to university that "all you're doing is paying".
- He also says administrators expense cars and gyms to the university ([27:00](https://youtu.be/hk_yhi3-prw?t=1620), verify).

### 5. Deliberate practice: "you'll succeed at anything"
[7:00](https://youtu.be/MX93U4KzA28?t=420) · heard it
- He cites K. Anders Ericsson: if you constantly study how you learn, you can succeed at anything.
- **Research:** Ericsson et al. 1993 is real. But a large meta-analysis found practice explains only about 12% of the differences in performance (Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014, *Psychological Science*, paper, not re-read). He overstates it.

### 6. Students as export products
[0:00](https://youtu.be/CbamEcNuDXo?t=0) and [12:00](https://youtu.be/0g3yo1DjiLM?t=720) · heard it
- Chinese students' goal is "to immigrate to a western country". Millions of Indian students are in Canada on student visas looking for permanent residence.
- **Link to Part D:** Indian students in Canada, a question very close to you.

### 7. Education as military training (Sparta)
[3:00](https://youtu.be/NpNCQ_gNqdE?t=180) · heard it
- Sparta built its whole education system to turn citizens into soldiers who could control the helots. It's the oldest example of a state designing school for its own needs.
