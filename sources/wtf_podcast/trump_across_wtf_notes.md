## 1. "The most effective political athlete we've ever seen" (Chamath, ~26:40–33:00)
**What's happening:** This is the "he's a businessman" idea you heard. Chamath's argument, in order:
1. COVID showed that trust in experts and credentials was fragile, and that the "experts" were mostly guessing.
2. So America needed a "vessel" for hard, structural change.
3. Trump is a "transcendent political athlete". He's best at one-on-one, rally-style connection, and his friendliness disarms people.
4. Because of his business background, he negotiates in a way career politicians can't match. Chamath says the same of Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.
5. America needed someone "willing to break the norms".

**Nikhil's reaction (an "oh, he's doing this" moment):** He pushes back gently: the debt is still rising, and Europeans at Davos looked uneasy. But first he says he's not sure how far he can debate this "without getting into trouble". So the host is self-censoring on air.

**The psychology:**
- **Why norm-breaking can feel "authentic":** When voters feel the system has lost its legitimacy, a candidate who openly breaks the rules (even by lying) comes across as an honest outsider. That's almost exactly Chamath's line.
  - Hahl, Kim & Zuckerman Sivan (2018), *The authentic appeal of the lying demagogue*, American Sociological Review *(covered last session; check what was read)*
- **Warmth plus competence:** We judge people on two things first, how warm they seem and how capable they seem. "Affable" and "great negotiator" hit both.
  - Fiske, Cuddy & Glick (2007), *Universal dimensions of social cognition*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences *(known, not re-read)*
- **Self-censorship:** People go quiet when they think their view could cost them (the "spiral of silence").
  - Noelle-Neumann (1974), *The spiral of silence*, Journal of Communication *(known, not re-read)*

**For balance:** Chamath is a vocal Trump supporter and has hosted a Trump fundraiser *(background, verify)*. Give his view the same scrutiny you'd give a Democratic donor praising a Democrat.

## 3. "My friends want the economy to crash, even if they lose money" (Ruchir, ~1:40:37)
**What's happening:** Many of Ruchir's friends want the US economy to tank because of Trump, even though they'd lose money. As an investor he can't afford to think that way. Economists predicted a recession from the tariffs, yet markets held up, partly because of the AI boom. His line to his friends: there's a force bigger than Trump, and it's AI.
**The psychology:** Partisan motivated reasoning: we read the economy through our political team.
- Kunda (1990), *The case for motivated reasoning*, Psychological Bulletin *(known, not re-read)*
- Bartels (2002), *Beyond the running tally*, Political Behavior: partisans see the same economy differently *(known, not re-read)*

## 4. "Nobody remembers anything": India's mood swings on Trump (Ruchir, ~2:13:50–2:15:30)
**What's happening:** When Trump won again there was euphoria in India: "he'll be like us". After Operation Sindoor, Indians were angry at China for arming Pakistan. Months later, the same people who attacked China were attacking Trump, and India was warming to China. Nikhil calls it human nature, and Ruchir agrees that nobody remembers anything.
**The psychology:** Public opinion often follows leaders and current events, not stable principles.
- Lenz (2012), *Follow the Leader?*, University of Chicago Press: voters often adopt their leader's positions rather than choosing leaders by policy *(known, not re-read)*

## 6. "Everybody lies" helps the real liars (Harari, ~23:18)
**What's happening:** Harari argues that the cynical view ("all politicians lie, it's all power games") hands a blank cheque to the actual liars.
**The psychology:** This is close to what researchers call the "liar's dividend": when people expect fakes and lies everywhere, real liars can wave away true evidence.
- Chesney & Citron (2019), *Deep fakes: a looming challenge for privacy, democracy, and national security*, California Law Review *(known, not re-read)*

## 10. Pharma: tariffs, research cuts, and Mankind's logo trick (WTF is Pharma)
**What's happening:**
- **The logo trick (~11:14):** In India, prescription drugs can't be advertised to the public. So Mankind launched consumer (OTC) products and ran ads that kept the Mankind logo on screen for over a second instead of the usual half-second. The goal was to make the company name familiar across India before its products reached each region. That's how a company builds a brand it isn't allowed to advertise.
- **Trump tariffs (~59:10):** Generic drugs aren't tariffed. But branded, patented drugs sold to the US and not made there may face a 100% tariff, which hits India's contract drug-manufacturing (CDMO) industry. The US BIOSECURE Act is also pushing companies away from Chinese suppliers ("China Plus One"), which helps India.
- **Research cuts (~1:08:49):** A guest says Trump has cut a lot of funding for public research. He quotes the National Cancer Institute at about $40B a year, which looks wrong: that's roughly the size of the whole NIH budget, and the NCI is a few billion *(background, verify)*.
- **"Our industry is ethical" (~2:07:55):** A guest says the top 10 Indian pharma companies do nothing wrong. **Counterpoint:** in the 2022 Dolo-650 case, tax officials alleged that the maker spent about ₹1,000 crore on freebies for doctors, and the company denied it *(background, verify)*. India brought in a marketing code (UCPMP) in 2024 *(background, verify)*.
- **"Every superhit product gets abused" (~2:00:32):** Said of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs. Patients pressure doctors into prescribing them.

**The psychology:**
- Mere exposure: seeing a name is enough to make people like it more. Zajonc (1968) *(known, not re-read)*
- Trump side (background, verify): in Sept 2025 Trump ordered a crackdown on misleading drug ads on US TV. The US and New Zealand are the only countries that allow prescription drug ads aimed at the public. In the same period came the Pfizer pricing deal and the TrumpRx site (from last session's notes).
