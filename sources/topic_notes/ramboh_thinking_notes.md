## 1. The Art of Conformity
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AeAlirH20oc · about 1 year old · example used: the Drake vs Kendrick Lamar feud
- **Main idea:** people decide who "won" by following the crowd, not by judging for themselves.
- **Why people act this way (his view):**
  - "It is part of our human nature to join the majority." If you see a crowd, you go and see what it's doing.
  - He shows the elevator/waiting-room conformity clip, where a woman keeps standing up at a beep even after the group has left. Once people learn a group rule, they keep following it when the group is gone.
  - **The numbers can be manufactured.** He says the record label (UMG) lifted copyright strikes only for Kendrick's track, so YouTubers could react to it and still get paid. That pushed the song everywhere, and people assumed "so many streams = the winner". He calls the stream counts "artificial".
  - Envy and morality also play a part. People wanted Drake to lose because they see him as arrogant and a bad person, and they think "I'm morally better, so why does he have the success?"
  - **The crowd wants a fight.** J. Cole said "the world want to see blood" and stepped out of the feud. Ramboh says every feed is built to show "the most intense forms of chaos" (fights, car crashes, war, political news) to distract you.
- **"The law of suggestion":** "the content you consume is a representation of who you are", and so are the people you surround yourself with.
- **Why people consume drama:** it's "noise" that distracts them from their own life problems, because those feel "too much to handle". He says the same is true of people who always have AirPods in. Watching drama feels like learning ("the art of war, delayed gratification") but is really procrastination.

## 2. The Art of Faking Stupid
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TVpGBm-D5zg · about 1 year old · examples used: IShowSpeed, OnlyFans creators

  - **Leverage** (he credits Naval Ravikant): success isn't how hard you work but where you put the effort, in something with "a high ceiling". The four types are labor, code, capital and media. Social media is the biggest because one performance reaches millions.
  - **Streamers who say "my job is easy" are lying to please their audience.** If it were easy, everyone would succeed at it. What's hard is figuring out leverage.
  - **Resources vs what you do with them:** NBA players are tall *and* the hardest workers *and* talented. Envy makes people see only the resource they lack.

## 7. The 7 Levels of Slavery
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cyJGo4gKEUM · about 1 year old
- **His definition:** "slavery is when someone has ownership over you… you can't do the things you want." His test: "can you do what you want without facing any consequences? If no, you are a slave."
- **His seven levels:**
  1. **Literal slavery:** being owned for life (it has happened to almost every race in history).
  2. **Location slavery:** where you're born decides your freedom. His example is North Korea, where you must worship the leader. People in rich countries should be grateful, because even a minimum wage there lets you save and leave.
  3. **Entertainment slavery:** you can't get off your phone, and there's always an AirPod or podcast in. "Many luxuries in life make you their slave": drugs, porn, games.
  4. **Social slavery:** "a societal construction that guilts you into doing something you are expected to do", such as going to college or meeting a partner's expectations. Stereotypes count too, both positive ("Asian = smart") and negative ("a man must act manly"). Break the rules and people start to dislike you.
  5. **Wage slavery:** money is "a sort of energy". It buys better doctors and therapists, but you can only get it by clocking in 9 to 5 for a manager. **YouTubers are slaves too:** they make what the audience wants, not what they want ("a social slave to what the audience wants").
  6. **Identity slavery:** a ready-made excuse label ("it's because I'm dumb", "because I'm a [group]"). It stops you learning from mistakes. People choose it because their unknown potential is scary: "I'd rather create a limit… I choose to be ignorant."
  7. **Sensory/genetic slavery:** our senses and brains are limited (bats use echolocation), and some people are born smarter or taller.
- **His conclusion:** levels 3–6 are the ones you can control. Being aware of them gives you more freedom.
