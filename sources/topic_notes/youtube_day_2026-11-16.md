## 1. Tom Nicholas: "Johnny Harris: A Story of YouTube Propaganda" (12 Mar 2021, 33 min, ~2.8M views) [watched: all]
**What he argues:**
- Johnny Harris's video *How China Became So Powerful* starts as a normal explainer, then turns into an argument for "stakeholder capitalism".
- Only in the **last minute** does Harris say it was made "in partnership with" the World Economic Forum (WEF), and he recommends a book by the WEF's founder, Klaus Schwab.
- A text version of the script appeared on the WEF website with a WEF communications executive, Peter Vanham, listed as an author. So Nicholas calls it an advert dressed as journalism.
- **Two kinds of sponsorship:**
  - an **"integration"** (a short ad break; the creator keeps control);
  - a **"sponsored post"** (the sponsor shapes the whole video).
  - The second is the problem, especially when the creator presents themselves as a journalist.
- **Wider claim:** governments and lobby groups are starting to use creators to push ideas. Examples: a *Times* report on China using British YouTubers, and a Chinese approach to the Canadian YouTuber J.J. McCullough.
- He is fair to Harris in one respect: he says it probably wasn't calculated, and that Harris may have been naive while finding his feet after leaving Vox.

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| A WEF article with the same argument, credited to Johnny Harris **and** Peter Vanham, appeared for Davos 2021 | ✅ The WEF page "After the rise of the West and Asia, we need a better form of capitalism" (Jan 2021) lists both [summary: search of weforum.org]. I couldn't confirm the author order Nicholas describes |
| Vanham was head of communications for the WEF chairman and co-wrote Schwab's *Stakeholder Capitalism* | ✅ A WEF press release lists him as "Head of Chairman's Communications"; the book is by Schwab with Vanham [summary] |
| The WEF is funded mostly by fees from big companies | ✅ Broadly true (partner and member fees) [summary; exact revenue figure not checked] ❓ |
| Harris disclosed the partnership only at the very end | ✅ Nicholas shows the clip; I didn't re-watch the Harris video myself ❓ |
| "This is propaganda" | ⚠️ **An opinion, not a fact.** Nicholas is openly left-wing and also critical of the WEF on political grounds. His *facts* (co-authorship, disclosure at the end) hold up. Whether this counts as "propaganda" depends on your view (Section 1.1) |

**Technique Nicholas himself uses:**
- He reads his own Surfshark ad *inside* a video about sponsorship ("this video isn't propaganda, but this part is sponsored").
- He's open about it, but it's the same "integration" he says is fine. That's a fair line, but worth noticing (compare Think School's fear-based sponsor read, overnight notes 2a).

## 2. Tom Nicholas: "Veritasium: A Story of YouTube Propaganda" (20 Oct 2021, 52 min, ~2.7M views) [watched: all]
**What he argues:**
- Veritasium's video *Why You Should Want Driverless Cars On Roads Now* (Jul 2021) was **paid for by Waymo** (Google's self-driving company). It was one of **five** creator videos made after the same Waymo briefing.
- The other creators repeat the **same talking points as if they were their own opinions**, the same "trusted messenger" effect our targeting and FOMO studies tested.
- His main point: **the video tells no outright lies; it misleads by what it leaves out and how it uses statistics.**

| Claim in the Veritasium video | Nicholas's correction | Check |
|---|---|---|
| "Human error causes 94% of crashes" (from the US road safety agency, NHTSA) | The 2015 NHTSA note said the driver was the "critical reason" (the last step before the crash) and that this was **not meant to blame the driver** | ✅ Nicholas is right. In 2022 the head of the NTSB (the US crash investigation board) publicly called the 94% figure misleading, and NHTSA changed its wording. IIHS estimated self-driving cars could prevent only about **a third** of crashes [summary] |
| Asiana Flight 214 (2013) shows humans fail where automation wouldn't | The pilots *were* using automation (the autothrottle); investigators blamed over-reliance on automation they didn't understand | ✅ The NTSB's acting chair, Christopher Hart, said automation had created **"new error types"**. Boeing said its systems worked as designed [summary] |
| Waymo has driven "20 million miles" | Mostly in sunny Phoenix suburbs; the cars need detailed pre-made maps and remote human support. Neither was mentioned | ✅ That was true in 2021 [summary from Nicholas quoting Waymo's own safety report] |
| Self-driving cars will reduce traffic | A car without a driver takes the same road space | ⚠️ Nicholas's reasoning; a fair point, but it's still debated |

**The other side, since 2021 (so the reader isn't left with only the critic):**
- **Waymo's safety record has improved a lot.**
  - A 2024 Waymo–Swiss Re study (25.3M driverless miles in Phoenix and San Francisco) found **88% fewer property-damage claims and 92% fewer injury claims** than human drivers.
  - Later reports cite even bigger figures over 200M+ miles.
  - **But** most of this data comes from Waymo itself or its partners, and critics want independent checks [summary].
- **So:** Nicholas was right that the 2021 video was one-sided and misused statistics. Waymo's later data suggests the technology itself may be safer than human drivers. **A misleading video can be pushing a conclusion that later turns out to be partly true.** Being right in the end doesn't make the method fair.
- **Muller's response:** I found no direct reply to Nicholas from Derek Muller (Veritasium's host). An AI-generated summary of a later interview says he would have made the video anyway because he believes in it; that's not reliable ❓.

**What this adds to the paper:**
- **Section 6.10 (corporations) and 6.11 (movies):** the Waymo campaign is the **civilian version of the Pentagon script deal**. A sponsor provides access (a car ride, staff briefings) and the creator produces a positive story. As with the films, the disclosure is there but brief.
- **Persuasion vs manipulation (Section 1.1):** a clear example of **"true facts, misleading picture"** (the trickery family in the persuasion notes). Nothing false, but chosen and framed to point one way.

## 3. *The Kerala Story* (2023): "inspired by true events" from both sides
**Background (checked):**
- The film's teaser claimed **32,000** Kerala women were converted and sent to ISIS.
- In **May 2023** the Supreme Court lifted West Bengal's ban on the film. It made the makers add disclaimers: there is **no authentic data** for 32,000 "or any other figure", and the film is a **fictionalised** account.
- The producer's lawyer agreed in court ✅ [summary: ThePrint, Newslaundry, Scroll].
- The trailer had already been changed from "32,000" to "3 girls" (RESEARCH.md A6).

### 3a. The Sham Sharma Show, "The Kerala Story Controversy | Propaganda Or Truth?" (9 May 2023, 18 min, ~1.1M) [watched: all] (right-leaning, defending the film)
**Main argument:** critics called the film propaganda before seeing it. Radicalisation in Kerala is real (Kerala women did join ISIS in Afghanistan), so the film's "story" is true even if it exaggerates.

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| Kerala women converted, married ISIS fighters and went to Afghanistan | ✅ Four Kerala women (Nimisha/Fathima, Sonia/Ayisha, Merrin/Mariyam, Rafeela) are in NIA records and Afghan jails (also in Rathee's video) |
| About 66 Indian-origin people joined ISIS (US State Department) | ✅ Both creators cite the same figure, from the US State Department's country reports |
| Kerala had the most pro-ISIS cases in India | ⚠️ Kerala is among the highest; ORF counts about 40 of 180–200 Indian cases. "Most" depends on how cases are counted |
| PFI was banned for radicalisation and terrorism | ✅ The central government banned PFI in Sept 2022 under the UAPA |
| Tipu Sultan forced 30,000 Brahmins out of Malabar | ⚠️ This comes from colonial-era sources, and historians debate the numbers |
| Textbooks hide the 1921 Moplah violence by calling it a "rebellion" | ⚠️ **A framing dispute:** historians describe both anti-British and anti-Hindu sides. Calling it only one or the other is a choice |
| The censor board ordered 10 cuts | ✅ |

**How it persuades:**
- **He admits he hasn't seen the film** ("To be honest, I haven't seen the film yet"), yet defends it. Abhijit Chavda did the same with *Dhurandhar* in the overnight notes.
- **He never mentions the 32,000 figure,** the main thing the film was criticised for. Leaving it out lets "some women joined ISIS" (true) stand in for "32,000 women" (no evidence).
- **Jokes and memes** (Rahul Gandhi clips, "simps", Iron Man) keep the tone light while a very serious claim about a whole religious group is made. Humour lowers guard (Section 4).
- **Whataboutism:** the BBC Modi documentary gets "free speech", so why not this film?
- **Generalising:** history and population figures are used to suggest a whole community is a threat.

### 3b. Dhruv Rathee, "The Kerala Story | True or Fake?" (10 May 2023, 22 min, ~25M) [watched: all, English track] (left-leaning, attacking the film)
**Main argument:** the film uses three propaganda techniques: the "Big Lie" (32,000), repetition and half-truths. The real number fitting the film's story is **3 women**.

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| The 32,000 figure has no basis; the director called it arbitrary, and the description changed to 3 | ✅ The Supreme Court-ordered disclaimer and the producer's own lawyer back this |
| The former CM Oommen Chandy's figure (2,667 conversions to Islam, 2006–12) never said "forced" or "ISIS" | ✅ Matches the 2012 India Today report [summary] |
| Of 6 Kerala women in the NIA list, 3 were already Muslim, 2 converted from Christianity, 1 from Hinduism | ❓ Plausible, from news reports; not checked against the NIA chargesheet |
| In 2018 the NIA examined 11 of about 90 interfaith marriage cases and found no forced conversion | ✅ Widely reported in Oct 2018. **But** it was based on unnamed NIA officials, and a 2017 Reuters report (also anonymous) said 9 cases were linked to ISIS recruiters [summary]. **The evidence is not as clean as Rathee says** |
| The government told Parliament (2020) that no "love jihad" case had been reported by central agencies | ✅ The MoS Home's written reply, Feb 2020 |
| India has far fewer ISIS recruits per person than France, Belgium or Jordan | ✅ Broadly right (India is very low per million). His exact ratios were not checked ❓ |
| Pew 2021: about 0.7% left Hinduism and about 0.8% joined it, so conversion is balanced | ✅ Matches Pew's 2021 *Religion in India* survey |
| The "Big Lie" was Hitler's technique, and Goebbels said to repeat a lie until people believe it | ⚠️ **Partly wrong.** In *Mein Kampf* Hitler *accused Jews* of using the "big lie"; he didn't recommend it. The famous Goebbels line has **no known source** and is listed as a fake quote by the historian Randall Bytwerk. Historians do say the Nazis used the method in practice [summary] |

**How it persuades:**
- **A Nazi comparison in the first minute** (Hitler and Goebbels) frames the film before any evidence is shown. It's the same loaded framing the overnight notes found in his *Dhurandhar* video.
- **The "urinating on planes" analogy** makes the 32,000-vs-3 gap feel absurd. It's effective and fair on the number, but it's still an emotional device.
- **He states the NIA finding more strongly than the evidence allows** and leaves out the 2017 Reuters report.
- **He ends with a positive "real Kerala story"** (India's low recruitment, de-radicalisation work, Kerala's development). It's a counter-story, not just a correction.
- **A strength:** he gives the **true kernel** (4–6 women, real radicalisation) instead of denying everything.

### 3c. What the two sides together show (my comparison)
- **Both agree on the kernel:** a small number of Kerala women really were radicalised and went to ISIS. They disagree on **scale** (thousands vs 3) and **meaning** (a conspiracy vs individual cases).
- **This is exactly how "inspired by real events" works:** a true kernel makes the invented scale and the conspiracy feel believable.
  - Sham Sharma's defence rests entirely on the kernel and skips the number.
  - The Supreme Court's fix was a **specific** disclaimer about the number, not only "this is fiction".
  - That's the Butler et al. (2009) finding: only specific warnings work. It's also what versions 4 and 5 of movies round 2 test.
- **Both sides leave things out:**
  - The right-leaning video drops the 32,000 claim.
  - The left-leaning video drops the 2017 Reuters report and leans on a misattributed Goebbels quote.
  - Rathee's checkable numbers hold up much better. That's the same pattern as the *Dhurandhar* videos overnight.
- **Neither creator had a reason to show a balanced picture:** both channels earn from their audience agreeing with them.

## 4. AI persuasion: David Rand on "DebunkBot" (*Pioneers of AI* podcast, 16 Jul 2025, 30 min) [watched: all]
*Why this one: for the paper's [TO WRITE] question [10] (AI persuasion), I wanted a researcher, not a commentator. The YouTube search results on "AI more persuasive than humans" were mostly tiny channels with no sources.*

**What he says:**
- Rand (MIT, now Cornell) **expected the study to fail**. He used to tell reporters that once people are down the "rabbit hole", trying to talk them out is a "lost cause".
- **In the experiment:**
  - People who believed a conspiracy wrote it down and rated it from 0 to 100.
  - They then had three short rounds of chat (about 6–7 minutes) with GPT-4, which used counter-evidence tailored to them.
  - A control group chatted with the AI about something unrelated.
- **The result:** belief fell by about **20% on average**, and it was still there **10 days and 2 months later**. About **75% of believers did not stop believing**: the effect is real but not total.
- **His explanation:** many believers have simply **never heard the other explanation** clearly. People avoid arguments, so believers also overestimate how many others agree with them.
- **Label test:** calling the bot "an expert" instead of "an AI" made **no difference**. Rand says what persuades is the **evidence**, not the AI label.
- **Other uses:**
  - **Brand-new conspiracies** (after the 2024 Trump shooting, from both the right and the left): it still worked, by saying "this is what's known; everything else is guessing".
  - **The racial wealth gap with Republicans:** support for structural explanations rose by about 15–20 points among those who first rejected them.

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| Science 2024, about 2,190 believers, about 20% drop, lasted 2 months | ✅ Costello, Pennycook & Rand, *Science* (Sept 2024), doi 10.1126/science.adq1814 [summary: abstract and MIT write-up]. It also reduced belief in *other* conspiracies the person hadn't discussed |
| "AI" vs "expert" label made no difference | ✅ A 2025 follow-up in *PNAS Nexus* [summary] |
| Worked on fresh conspiracies too | ✅ A 2026 arXiv preprint (CMU, MIT and Cornell) on the Trump-shooting and Charlie Kirk-shooting conspiracies: chat beat a static fact sheet. The authors warn it could wrongly "debunk" a **real** conspiracy [summary] |
| WTC 7 collapsed after debris from the towers started fires; an "NTSB investigation" showed this | ⚠️ Right on the cause (fires started by debris), **but he named the wrong agency**: it was NIST, not the NTSB. A small slip |

**The other side (important for the paper):**
- **The same skill can push either way.** Salvi et al. 2025 (*Nature Human Behaviour*, about 900 US participants) found that GPT-4 debating people **with access to their age, gender and politics** won **64.4% of the debates where one side won** [summary].
  - Without that personal information, it did about as well as humans.
  - ⚠️ Many news sites wrongly reported this as "64% more persuasive".
- **Rand's own studies used AI pointed at the truth.** I found **no published study** testing the same method pointed at a falsehood (the reverse of DebunkBot), probably for ethical reasons ❓. This is an open gap, not proof that it's safe.
- **Link to our project:** this is the healthcare and targeting finding in a new form. **Personalised + patient + evidence-based = powerful.** Whether that power is "persuasion" or "manipulation" depends on whether the evidence is true and whether the person knows they're being persuaded (Section 1.1 notes: hidden influence and trickery).

## 5. A fairness test: Dhruv Rathee on the Emergency, "Indira Gandhi's Emergency | Why it happened?" (25 Sept 2021, 17 min, ~12M) [watched: all, English track]
*Why this one: most of the Indian propaganda examples in the project are from the BJP era (Dhurandhar, The Kerala Story). This video looks at Congress's darkest period, and tests whether a creator often called anti-BJP is equally hard on Congress.*

**What he says:**
- Most of the video (about 12 of 17 minutes) explains the **causes**:
  - Indira Gandhi's fights with the courts (bank nationalisation, privy purses);
  - power being centralised; the 1971 war and the 1973 oil shock causing inflation;
  - the Gujarat and Bihar movements; George Fernandes's railway strike;
  - the Allahabad High Court judgement (guilty on 2 of 14 charges, seat cancelled);
  - JP Narayan urging police and soldiers to refuse orders.
- **The Emergency itself** gets about 3 minutes:
  - over 100,000 arrested; RSS and Jamaat-e-Islami banned; elections delayed;
  - Sanjay Gandhi's forced sterilisation of "some men";
  - "trains ran on time"; then the 1977 defeat.
- **His verdict:** "the darkest period of Indian democracy".

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| Convicted on 2 of 14 grounds (use of state machinery for the stage; Yashpal Kapoor still a government employee) | ✅ Matches the standard account of the Raj Narain case |
| Over 100,000 people arrested | ✅ Roughly. The Shah Commission recorded about 35,000 under MISA plus about 76,000 under the Defence of India rules, about 111,000 in total [summary] |
| Forced sterilisation of "some men" | ⚠️ **A big understatement.** About **10.7 million** sterilisations in 1975–77 (over 8 million in 1976–77 alone), above the government's own target. The Shah Commission recorded **1,774 deaths** linked to the procedures [summary: government figures cited by Deccan Herald; PIB] |
| Power to Delhi newspaper offices was cut on the night of 25 June | ✅ [summary] |
| The 1974 railway strike was the "largest industrial strike in the world" | ❓ Often said; not checked |

**What the video leaves out (it matters for our topic):**
- **Pre-censorship of all media** and **over 250 journalists jailed** [summary] are barely mentioned. So are the propaganda side of the Emergency (compulsory government newsreels and the 20-point programme) and the destruction of the film ***Kissa Kursi Ka*** (RESEARCH.md A6).
- The ban on **Kishore Kumar's songs on All India Radio**, reported after he refused to sing for the Youth Congress, isn't mentioned ❓ (widely told, not checked today).

**How it persuades (compared with his *Kerala Story* video):**
- The **Kerala Story** video (BJP-aligned film) opens with Hitler and Goebbels. The **Emergency** video (Congress) opens with a neutral question ("why did it happen?") and spends most of its time on the **pressures Indira faced**.
- Both are partly fair. Here he does call it the darkest period, and mentions arrests, bans and sterilisation. **But the tone differs:**
  - for the side he dislikes, the framing comes first;
  - for the other side, the context comes first.
- **He also turns to the present:** while describing Indira's centralisation, he adds that the same accusation is "levied on Prime Minister Narendra Modi nowadays".
- **This is an example of framing by structure:** *how much time* goes to causes vs harms, and *what comes first*, shapes the verdict without any false fact.
- **To be fair to him:** the video is from 2021 and calls itself a "why it happened" history, so focusing on causes fits the title. **The pattern only shows up when you compare videos.** A one-video fact-check would miss it.
- It's similar to what the overnight notes found with the four *Dhurandhar* creators: facts mostly fine, framing chosen.

## 6. *Scare Out* abroad: does the foreign marketing say who backed it? [read: official UK trailer page and "Making of" page descriptions; no English transcript available]
- **The fact (checked):** *Scare Out* (dir. Zhang Yimou, released 17 Feb 2026) is the **first Chinese film publicly backed by the Ministry of State Security (MSS)**.
  - The MSS said it should "strengthen the people's defence line" and serve "national security education".
  - Zhang wrote in the *People's Daily* that security officers were with the crew **throughout filming**.
  - ✅ AP, 15 Mar 2026 [summary: AP via The Star and others]. This confirms Part F3.
- **What the UK distributor (Trinity CineAsia) says on YouTube:**
  - The official UK & Ireland trailer description presents it as a thriller in which "a squad of China's National Security" hunts spies leaking fighter-jet secrets.
  - The "Making of" description is only about camera style (handheld, drones).
  - **Neither mentions that the MSS backed the film** [read: both descriptions, 16 Nov].
- **Why it matters:**
  - The agency's role was public **inside China** (the MSS promoted it itself). But for **foreign audiences** the film is sold as just a Zhang Yimou thriller.
  - This is the **same gap as Hollywood's Pentagon films**: the credit is there if you look, but not where most viewers would see it.
  - Our movies round 1 found that a clear "the ministry approved this" notice cancelled the film's trust boost. No country makes this kind of notice compulsory.
- ❓ I couldn't check the UK cinema prints or end credits, so the MSS may be credited inside the film itself.

## 7. *The Kashmir Files* (2022): another "based on true events" film, two explainers
*Why: like The Kerala Story, it was promoted as a true story, made tax-free in several BJP-run states and praised by the PM, and attacked as propaganda (the 2022 IFFI jury head Nadav Lapid called it "vulgar propaganda"). Here, unlike Kerala, the core event is undisputed. The fight is over **scale, words and blame**.*

### 7a. Nitish Rajput, "Kashmiri Pandit Exodus" (22 Mar 2022, 19 min, ~4.3M) [watched: all, English captions]
- **He sets the terms up front:** "I've said exodus and not genocide... decide from your own understanding at the end", and asks viewers to put their ideology aside.
- **The story (1983–90):**
  - Congress splitting Farooq Abdullah's party;
  - Ghulam Mohammad Shah's religious politics and the 1986 riots;
  - Rajiv Gandhi opening the Babri Masjid locks;
  - the **rigged 1987 election**, which pushed MUF candidates into militancy (Syed Salahuddin);
  - **Pakistan reusing the US–Afghan jihad model** (money, arms, training);
  - the JKLF's targeted killings of prominent Pandits;
  - the Rubaiya Sayeed kidnapping and release of militants;
  - 19 January 1990 with no government in Srinagar;
  - the exodus; the Gawkadal firing on Muslim protesters on 21 January.
- **His blame:** **every** government. Congress (1984–87), Farooq Abdullah, V.P. Singh's BJP-supported government (no army sent, militants released) and the current government (promised land and housing not delivered: "Kashmiri Pandits will remain in speeches, not in their homes").

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| The 1987 election was rigged and pushed MUF leaders into militancy | ✅ Widely accepted by historians and officials |
| Official deaths: 89 (RTI, J&K police) vs 219 (government); survivors say thousands | ✅ The J&K government told the Assembly in 2010 that **219** Pandits were killed between 1989 and 2004. The **89** RTI figure covered only from 1990 (and some say only Srinagar district) [summary]. He explains *why* the numbers differ, which is good practice |
| Gawkadal: 28 dead (official), about 50 (rights groups), 280 (survivors) | ✅ Matches the usual range of reports |
| Retired judge "Neelkanth Kajloo" killed in October 1989 | ⚠️ The name is **Neelkanth Ganjoo**; the probe was reopened in 2022 [summary]. Small errors in name and date |
| About 1.5 lakh Hindus and 18,735 Muslims left | ❓ Figures vary widely by source |
| Farooq Abdullah released 70 militants in 1989 | ❓ Claimed often, disputed; not checked |

### 7b. Akash Banerjee (The Deshbhakt), "Kashmir Files | The Hard Truth Or Vulgar Political Propaganda" (14 Mar 2022, 13 min, ~1.4M) [watched: all, English captions] (left-leaning)
- **The same history, almost point for point:** Congress's interference, 1987, Rubaiya Sayeed ("had VP Singh's government not surrendered...") and the targeted killings (Taploo, Ganjoo, Prem Nath Bhat).
- **Jagmohan:** he gives both sides. Some say Jagmohan encouraged Pandits to leave; others say he gave them a safe passage. "Both statements have elements of truth."
- **Pandit groups' figures:** about 70,000 left in 1990–92 and about 400 were killed, 1990–2011 (KPSS). That's higher than the government's 219.
- **His blame:** all parties ("in 30 years all parties got the chance"), and he names the BJP's 2014 promise as unfulfilled.
- **His main charge:** the tragedy is now used to show "who to hate".
- **Notably, the video says almost nothing about the film itself,** despite the title.

### 7c. What the two together show (my comparison)
- **On facts, the two creators agree far more than their audiences probably think.** One is often seen as neutral or nationalist (Rajput), the other as left-leaning (Banerjee). Both blame **all** governments and Pakistan, both describe the 1987 rigging and the targeted killings, and both give Gawkadal.
- **The critic even used the *higher* death toll** (Pandit groups' 400) rather than the government's 219. That's the opposite of what you'd expect if he were downplaying it.
- **The real fight is over words and use:**
  - "exodus" vs "genocide";
  - whether the film's purpose is remembering victims or spreading blame onto Kashmiri Muslims today.
  - Those are **framing** questions. Our round 2 test can't settle them, but it can test the mechanism: does "inspired by real events" make the **invented** parts (here: specific scenes and blame) feel as true as the real core?
- **The same pattern as *Kerala Story* and *Dhurandhar*: a true, painful kernel + a fiction label or no label + a political moment = a fight about everything except the facts.**
