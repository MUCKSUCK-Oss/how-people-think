## 1. "Wine is good for you" was marketing [0:15](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=15) and [22:59](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=1379)
- A guest says alcohol "has been marketed all too well", especially the "wine is good for you" message of the last 20 years, which he calls "absolutely bogus". He believes alcohol is as bad as smoking.
- **Research:** this matches the current consensus. The WHO said in January 2023 that "no level of alcohol consumption is safe for our health" ✔, and the old "moderate drinking is protective" studies had design problems (Stockwell et al. 2016 meta-analysis **[CHECK]**).
- **"Oh, they're doing this":** a health halo created by marketing and weak studies. This is Bernays's method of borrowing science's authority (the gelatine and bacon cases).
- **Good for the paper:** the producers put the clip at the very start of the episode, so it's also how the show hooks viewers.

## 2. Supplements: "Can it do any harm? If not, it's a check mark" [56:50–58:20](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=3410)
- A guest describes his rule for supplements: if it can't do harm, try it. Then he immediately warns you must measure yourself, because "you don't know what it is affecting".
- Another guest calls supplements "a crutch" for a short time, not a lifetime habit.
- At [2:03:15](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=7395) one guest says he takes "a bunch of supplements" and has "no idea whether [they're] doing something good for me or not", but checks his blood every 3 months.
- At [1:54:42](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=6882) another says of his routine: "I don't know if it's working or not."
- **Compare with Report 2:** the doctor on the Longevity episode said the supplement industry runs on "take it, it won't hurt". Here wealthy, intelligent guests openly follow exactly that logic, which shows how well the framing works.
- **Psychology:** low perceived risk plus a possible upside makes trying feel rational (an action bias). The placebo effect makes it feel like it works.

## 3. A sponsor-style disclaimer before testosterone supplements [53:05–53:38](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=3185)
- Nikhil introduces "natural herbs" that could raise testosterone, then adds: "none of us are doctors. This is not medical advice… go consult your doctor."
- **"Oh" note:** "natural" sounds safe, and a disclaimer lets the claim be made while appearing responsible. This links to the "natural" health halo and to Le Bon's point that a disclaimer doesn't stop the message landing.

## 4. Fat was vilified, which let sugar in [1:06:43–1:06:59](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=4003)
- A guest says fat "was vilified for many decades" and that this caused harm by letting sugar become a big part of people's diets.
- **Real history:** in the 1960s the Sugar Research Foundation paid Harvard scientists to play down sugar's role in heart disease and blame fat (Kearns, Schmidt & Glantz 2016, *JAMA Internal Medicine* ✔: about $6,500 at the time, an undisclosed review in *NEJM* in 1967. Critics say the paper overstates its long-term impact).
- **A strong case for the paper:** an industry funding science to shift blame. It's Bernays's "foot protection bureau" at national scale.

## 5. Sugary "health drinks" for children [1:23:24](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=5004)
- A guest mentions the "so-called health drinks" parents give young children, and how people are only now realising how much sugar they contain.
- **Indian context:** in 2024 the government told e-commerce sites to remove Bournvita and similar drinks from the "health drinks" category ✔ (Commerce Ministry advisory, 10 April 2024, after an NCPCR inquiry; the viral sugar video was by influencer Revant Himatsingka in 2023).
- **Psychology:** a label is a frame. Calling a product a "health drink" sets a default belief.

## 6. Who decides what's healthy? [1:27:48–1:28:20](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=5268)
- On sweeteners: guests discuss a WHO statement on sweeteners being "misrepresented" by the media, and ask whether lobbying played a role.
- **Paper use:** the public meets science second-hand through media framing (Lippmann).

## 7. Glamorised misinformation: "sleep when I'm dead" [1:40:06](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=6006)
- A guest calls "you live only once… I'll sleep when I'm dead" culture "misinformation that is almost glamorized", which many young people buy into.
- At [1:34:45](https://youtu.be/6HE6d0lKh4o?t=5685): people overwork because of "the insecurity of not wanting to look like they're not working 20 hours a day".
- **Psychology:** social norms and signalling. Hustle culture works as social proof.
