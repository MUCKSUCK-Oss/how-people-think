## Part A. The classic playbook

### 1. Machiavelli, twice
**Nikhil and Ep 13** ([36:00](https://youtu.be/JjDjDvNgkFo?t=2160)): a prince must look generous, religious and moral. Real generosity backfires: people get used to it, taxes rise to pay for it, and they turn on him.

**The UAE's Omar Al Olama** ([26:47–32:56](https://youtu.be/9gX2-RvNhf8?t=1607), heard it):
- Asked whether fear or love keeps a kingdom longer, he says manipulators "don't survive for long".
- "You can trick many people for a short time, or a few for a long time, but not many for a long time." (The line is often credited to Lincoln, but that attribution is doubtful.)
- Social media exposes anyone who puts on an act.
- **The "oh, he's doing this" moment:** he then says the family that has ruled Dubai since 1833 has never had a single member who "puts on an act". A serving minister telling millions of viewers that his own ruler is authentic is image management in action, and nobody outside can check it.
- **Research:** we trust a message more when it goes *against* the speaker's own interest (Eagly, Wood & Chaiken 1978, paper, not re-read). This one goes with it.

### 2. "The algorithm wants war"
- **Nas Daily, Ep 13** ([50:00](https://youtu.be/JjDjDvNgkFo?t=3000)):
  - "More leaders died making peace than war": Sadat (1981) and Rabin (1995). Nas says Rabin was "in Palestine". He was Israel's prime minister.
  - His own video calling for peace got about 100 death threats.
- **Harari** ([42:19](https://youtu.be/N0S048D2tj4?t=2539)): we handed public conversation to algorithms built to maximize engagement, and anger engages more than compassion.
- **Jiang** ([2:05:42](https://youtu.be/A9Sr-4c-3Tg?t=7542)): the elite rule by spreading "hatred, fear and anxiety". Most people agree on 99% of issues, and the media inflates the 1% "for engagement".
- **Research:**
  - Each moral-emotional word in a tweet raised retweets by about 20% (Brady et al. 2017, *PNAS*).
  - Posts attacking the other side get shared most (Rathje et al. 2021).
  - People think the other side is more extreme than it is, the "perception gap" (More in Common 2019; Moore-Berg et al. 2020, *PNAS*).
  - All papers, not re-read.

### 5. Bollywood shapes how we see the police

### 6. Good persuasion: Wilberforce's three arguments
Rishi Sunak: [2:12:29](https://youtu.be/spdUv7OFOu4?t=7949) · heard it
- Wilberforce's own reason for opposing slavery was moral, but he "recognised that wasn't the best way to persuade everyone".
- So he also argued it on economic grounds, and on patriotic grounds (slaves supplied to France would help France beat Britain).
- Thomas Clarkson collected the evidence behind it.
- **Research:** arguments framed in the *listener's* moral values persuade more, called moral reframing (Feinberg & Willer 2015, 2019, paper, not re-read).
- **Your takeaway:** the same technique can serve a good cause.

- **Research:** the rally-round-the-flag effect (Mueller 1970, paper, not re-read).

## Part C. How a YouTube prophet persuades: Predictive History as a case study

This is the most "oh, he's doing this" part of the whole project. Jiang uses persuasion techniques on his own audience:

1. **Advertising his hits:** "I predicted Trump would win, the Iran war, and that the US would lose" ([1:53:17](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=6797)). He tells the audience he's "fairly convinced" Trump watched his video ([1:12:05](https://youtu.be/5gyyAzfmWk4?t=4325)).
   - Research: we remember the hits and forget the misses (hindsight bias, Fischhoff 1975). Track records need counting, not anecdotes (Tetlock 2005).
2. **One test far in the future:** "If Trump doesn't get a third term, ignore me." It sounds testable, but it stays open for years, and meanwhile everything else he says gets accepted.
3. **"Just connecting dots":** "I don't know what is happening. I'm just trying to draw these points" ([1:03:03](https://youtu.be/N2GFDDZtGQY?t=3783)). Harvard to Zuckerberg to Kushner to Chabad to Adelson to Netanyahu.
   - Research: seeing patterns in random links predicts conspiracy belief (van Prooijen, Douglas & De Inocencio 2018, paper, not re-read).
4. **Admitting the soundbite habit:** "I can be very extreme… sometimes I prefer the sound bite… a bold statement that goes viral" ([8:22](https://youtu.be/A9Sr-4c-3Tg?t=502)). He then promises to be more balanced.
5. **"Red pill" and secret knowledge:** "Take the red pill." Teach kids the occult and Kabbalah. The elite reach "other spiritual beings" ([2:31:18](https://youtu.be/YyUl1QerHjA?t=9078)). He claims he can sense people's thoughts from far away ([2:00:02](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=7202)).
6. **Denying he's a cult while acting like a prophet:** "I don't want to start a cult… I'm not doing this for fame, money or power" ([1:49:44](https://youtu.be/3xwJFjWcFow?t=6584)).
7. **Using his own life story:** a substitute teacher told him he was "special", which led to Yale on a full scholarship. His father washed dishes for 40 years. Personal stories lower the listener's guard.
   - Research: people persuaded through stories are less likely to argue back, called narrative transportation (Green & Brock 2000, paper, not re-read).

## Part D. ⚠ Warning: old prejudice in new clothes

Parts of the channel repeat known antisemitic and "replacement" conspiracy patterns. Recognize them; never quote them as fact:
- "The Jewish diaspora… the most effective mechanism of transnational capital… I'm not saying this as an anti-Semitic comment" ([1:24:02](https://youtu.be/Z1G6WI1Q0M8?t=5042)).

- The Rothschild–Waterloo legend ([0:45:33](https://youtu.be/5rDMq-5_xkM?t=2733)). It comes from an 1846 antisemitic pamphlet and historians call it false.
- "Population replacement" in Canada ([0:44:36](https://youtu.be/hcuT_mpeTxo?t=2676)).
- Ethnic generalizations: Chinese people "not imaginative" and "not a threat" ([2:13:32](https://youtu.be/q7GdLK1xwAo?t=8012), [2:23:18](https://youtu.be/q7GdLK1xwAo?t=8598)).

**The psychology:** conspiracy antisemitism grows when people feel they have lost control over politics (Kofta, Soral & Bilewicz 2020, *JPSP*, paper, not re-read). Each claim follows the same pattern: a hidden group, secret coordination, and an explanation for everything. The disclaimer "I'm not saying this as an anti-Semitic comment" is itself a known move, called a disclaimer before prejudice.
