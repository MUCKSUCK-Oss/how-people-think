# WTF Ep #2: Secrets of Social Media Success (SkU3J2vWUK8)
Read on 6 Nov 2026 via the YouTube transcript panel. The full transcript is 1,221 lines. I read only the passages matching manipulation, choice and psychology keywords (266 lines). Guests: Nikhil Kamath with three social-media insiders (a co-founder of Dailyhunt/Josh, a top creator and a live-streaming founder). Guest names are not confirmed from the transcript; check them on the video page before naming anyone.

Link format: https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=SECONDS

## 1. Likes on comments "changed the game" [3:13](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=193)
- A guest says that when Facebook added likes to comments, and Twitter replaced copy-paste "RT" with a retweet button, likes became everyone's game, not only the poster's.
- He adds that social media is "all designed around the human brain".
- **Psychology:** variable social rewards. Each like is a small reward, which keeps people posting.
- **Simulation link:** the fake popularity and herding tricks.

## 2. The "dopamine hit at both ends" business model [7:34–8:50](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=454)
- A guest walks through the maths: the user gets a dopamine hit from likes and keeps coming back; every foot of feed carries an ad. Ten minutes a day across a billion users is billions of dollars.
- He calls it "a vicious cycle of a dopamine hit at both ends": the user's pleasure and the platform's revenue feed each other.
- At [32:22](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=1942): "the incentive model is very simple: more users, get them to spend more time… it results in ad revenue."
- **"Oh, they're doing this":** the platform's goal is time spent, not your good choices.

## 3. Hunting for your tribe [11:41](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=701)
- Validation used to come from one tribe. Now people belong to many and search every comment section for "people who think like you", which is why they chase likes and retweets.
- **Psychology:** need to belong (Baumeister & Leary 1995), homophily.
- **Simulation link:** echo chambers.

## 4. Cambridge Analytica and showing the "other side" to provoke you [25:09–25:49](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=1509)
- Nikhil asks whether the negative feedback loop is bigger than the positive one: would a right-winger be shown pro-left content just to make him react?
- A guest points to Cambridge Analytica as proof that this kind of manipulation happened, and says he is "philosophically dead against" it.
- **Check:** what Cambridge Analytica actually achieved is debated. Researchers doubt its "psychographic targeting" worked as claimed. Put it in the paper as a claim, with that caveat.
- Rage bait is linked to research on out-group animosity driving shares (Rathje et al. 2021, in Report 4).

## 5. A founder's pitch: "no social graph, no personal data" [27:45](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=1665) and [32:22](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=1942)
- The Dailyhunt/Josh co-founder says his platform lets users stay anonymous, never asks for personal data, and personalises on a "content graph" instead of friends. He says this was done "very consciously to avoid this confirmation bias".
- **Balance note:** this is a founder describing his own product, so it's a sales pitch as well as an argument. Hold it to the same standard as any company claim.

## 6. Influencers first: top-down growth [29:39](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=1779)
- New platforms grow "top down": bring ministers, sportsmen and influencers on board first, and the public follows.
- **Bernays link:** this is exactly his "key men" method from 1928 (win the group leaders, and their followers come).

## 7. "TikTok's looking straight back into you" [42:50–44:54](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=2570)
- The feed reads your "implied preferences" from how long you watch, like, share and comment, so every feed is different and "you could wail away hours".
- Nikhil says he does the same endless scrolling on a food-delivery app.
- At [1:49:54](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=6594): TikTok is said to have the most sophisticated content-graph algorithm, so feeds are "so engaging to that individual".
- **Psychology:** the infinite feed removes stopping cues (a default to keep going).
- **Simulation link:** a default; nobody chooses to stop.

## 8. Negativity spreads more [51:44](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=3104) and [2:02:06](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=7326)
- "People come together on the negative a lot more than the positive."
- One guest estimates that on Twitter about 6 of 10 comments are negative, versus about 1 in 10 on Facebook. He says a platform's culture and feed design shape the tone.
- **Psychology:** negativity bias (Baumeister et al. 2001; Rozin & Royzman 2001).

## 9. "Envy is the fuel for social media" [2:04:49–2:07:19](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=7489)
- A guest calls envy "the fuel for social media". Much of the hate for influencers and CEOs is envy.
- **A very "oh" moment:** he admits that if he goes to a popular creator's page and says something negative, he knows other envious people will like it, so negativity is rewarded with likes. Example: the top replies to Elon Musk's tweets try to "dunk" on him.
- Instagram makes people feel worst, then Twitter ([2:00:22](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=7222)). People compare their lives with others' highlight reels.
- **Psychology:** upward social comparison (Festinger 1954); Instagram and well-being studies.

## 10. "For You" turned his feed into an echo chamber [2:00:54](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=7254)
- A guest says Twitter's "For You" tab made his feed "an echo chamber" of things he once thought he wanted. He doesn't even read it anymore; he just scrolls.
- **Simulation link:** echo chambers plus confirmation bias (v2, Section 5.4 of the paper).

## 11. Founders admit the harm [2:31:08–2:35:07](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=9068)
- Nikhil says it seems "pretty clear" that heavy social media isn't good for mental well-being, then asks the guests about "an ethical conundrum": they own the platforms.
- A founder agrees the impact on children is "very, very deeply negative", from aspiration to body shaming, and describes AI moderation against harmful content.
- **Research:** the size of the social-media harm to teens is debated. Haidt (*The Anxious Generation*, 2024) argues it's large; Orben & Przybylski (2019) find small average associations. Present both sides.

## 12. Authenticity as a skill [1:32:17](https://youtu.be/SkU3J2vWUK8?t=5537)
- "Emotionally connecting with an audience… really comes down to authenticity": being comfortable with your flaws being public.
- **"Oh" note:** authenticity described as a skill to master is itself a persuasion tool. This links to Report 1 (the "authentic rule-breaker").
