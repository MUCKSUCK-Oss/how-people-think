# Bibliography v1 (2026-10-25)

## A. Does persuasion work? (effects and limits)

**Kalla & Broockman (2018).** The minimal persuasive effects of campaign contact in general elections: Evidence from 49 field experiments. *American Political Science Review*, 112(1), 148–166.
- Question: do campaign contact and ads change general-election candidate choice?
- Finding: best estimate is about zero in general elections; rare exceptions (very unpopular positions, very early contact that fades).
- Limits: US general elections, mostly contact (mail, canvass), not mass media.
- Access: Abstract. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055417000363 (CHECK DOI) | https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/apsrev/v112y2018i01p148-166_00.html
- Relevance: anchors the "weak under competition" half of your thesis.

**Coppock, Hill & Vavreck (2020).** The small effects of political advertising are small regardless of context, message, sender, or receiver. *Science Advances*, 6(36), eabc4046. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abc4046
- Question: do small average ad effects hide large effects in specific conditions?
- Finding: 59 experiments, ~34,000 people, 49 ads (2016): effects small and similar across contexts. Small effects can still matter in close races.
- Limits: 2016 US only; individual ads, not whole campaigns.
- Access: Abstract (open access at PMC7467695, not downloaded).
- Relevance: same as above; rules out the "hidden heterogeneity" excuse.

**Mercier (2017).** How gullible are we? A review of the evidence from psychology and social science. *Review of General Psychology*, 21(2), 103–122. https://doi.org/10.1037/gpr0000111
- Finding: propaganda, campaigns and advertising are generally weak; acceptance mostly reflects prior beliefs. People may still be vulnerable to novel forms of manipulation.
- Access: Abstract only (full text restricted).
- Relevance: strongest counterweight to a "mass manipulation" framing.

**Mercier. Not Born Yesterday.** Princeton University Press. Year not shown on the page I saw (CHECK).
- Finding: people are rationally skeptical; mass persuasion mostly fails. Access: Summary (publisher essay). https://press.princeton.edu/node/42455

**Chong & Druckman (2007).** Framing public opinion in competitive democracies. *American Political Science Review*, 101(4), 637–655.
- Finding: frame quality matters more than frequency; competition changes but does not eliminate framing effects; single-frame studies overstate power.
- Access: Abstract. https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/apsrev/v101y2007i04p637-655_07.html

**DellaVigna & Gentzkow (2010).** Persuasion: Empirical evidence. *Annual Review of Economics*, 2, 643–669.
- Question: how much does persuasion affect consumers, voters, donors, investors?
- Access: Abstract. https://www.nber.org/papers/w15298
- Relevance: closest existing cross-sector review; economics-focused and pre-social-media.

**Tappin, Wittenberg, Hewitt, Berinsky & Rand (2023).** Quantifying the potential persuasive returns to political microtargeting. *PNAS*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10288628
- Finding: tailoring to one attribute made ads about 70% more effective than the single best ad; no extra gain from several attributes; benefits varied a lot across the two policies tested and were smaller in the second experiment.
- Limits: online survey experiment, not real campaigns; authors note funding of related work by Google and Meta.
- Access: Summary (MIT News https://news.mit.edu/2023/study-microtargeting-politics-tailored-ads-0621). PMC page blocked by a bot check.

**Garthwaite & Moore.** The role of celebrity endorsements in politics: Oprah, Obama, and the 2008 Democratic primary. *Journal of Law, Economics, and Organization*. Volume/year CHECK.
- Finding: estimated about 1 million additional votes for Obama; no link to earlier elections or local political preferences.
- Limits: one unusual celebrity and a primary; authors call her a case of "nearly unparalleled influence."
- Access: Abstract. https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/the_oprah_effect/

## B. When influence is strong (control of information, priors)

**Adena, Enikolopov, Petrova, Santarosa & Zhuravskaya (2015).** Radio and the rise of the Nazis in prewar Germany. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, pp. 1885–1939. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv030
- Finding: radio slowed Nazi support when it carried pro-government news; effect reversed after the Nazis took control; propaganda worked most where antisemitism was historically high and backfired where it was low.
- Access: Abstract. Relevance: control of channel + matching priors.

**Yanagizawa-Drott.** Propaganda and conflict: Evidence from the Rwandan genocide. Harvard CID Working Paper 257 (published version CHECK).
- Finding: radio coverage increased participation in violence; about 9–10% of participation attributed (figure differs by version). Effects decreased with literacy.
- Access: Abstract. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cid:wpfacu:257

**Peisakhin & Rozenas (2018).** Electoral effects of biased media: Russian television in Ukraine. *American Journal of Political Science*, 62(3), 535–550. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12355
- Finding: Russian TV increased pro-Russian votes overall; largest on voters with pro-Russian priors; weaker or counterproductive for pro-Western viewers; deeper polarization.
- Access: Abstract.

**Ye et al. (2026)?** Meta-analysis of the illusory truth effect (182 studies, 31,184 participants; small average effect after adjusting for small-study effects). Authors/venue CHECK: I saw only a glossary page and a Nature Communications supplementary file name. https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/illusion-of-truth-effect

**Dechêne, Stahl, Hansen & Wänke (2010).** The truth about the truth: A meta-analytic review of the truth effect. (Journal CHECK.) Reported medium effect (d = 0.53). Seen only through citations in other papers (Not read).

## C. Distraction, attention and information control

**King, Pan & Roberts (2017).** How the Chinese government fabricates social media posts for strategic distraction, not engaged argument. *American Political Science Review*.
- Finding: ~448 million fabricated posts a year; they almost never argue with critics; they distract with positive content.
- Access: Abstract + paper PDF page seen. https://cyberir.mit.edu/?p=938

**Roberts (2020).** *Censored: Distraction and Diversion Inside China's Great Firewall.* Princeton University Press.
- Finding: censorship works through fear, friction, and flooding; most citizens are deterred by friction and flooding. Also suggests relevance to democracies.
- Access: Summary. https://newbooksnetwork.com/margaret-e-roberts-censored-distraction-and-diversion-inside-chinas-great-firewall-princeton-up-2020

**Lewandowsky, Jetter & Ecker (2020).** Using the president's tweets to understand political diversion in the age of social media. *Nature Communications*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-19644-6 (DOI inferred from supplement file name, CHECK)
- Finding: more ABC Mueller coverage associated with more Trump tweets on jobs/China/immigration, associated with less next-day NYT coverage; placebo topics showed no pattern.
- Limits: first two years only; two outlets; keyword choice involved judgment; associations, intent unclear.
- Access: Summary (University of Bristol press release https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2020/november/trump-twitter.html).

**"Distracting from the Epstein files?" (arXiv:2511.11532, Nov 2025).** Preprint, not peer reviewed; authors CHECK. Finds patterns consistent with diversion within friendly media; authors say observational, no formal causal identification. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11532v1

**Guriev & Treisman (2022).** *Spin Dictators.* Princeton University Press.
- Argument: many modern autocrats rule by shaping information and simulating democracy rather than by mass violence; discusses parallels with populists in democracies.
- Access: Summary. https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/3924
