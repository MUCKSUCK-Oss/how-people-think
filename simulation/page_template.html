What this model can and can't tell you

  • Every trick's strength is an assumption, tuned so the result lands near a real experiment. The model shows how mechanisms work and combine. It does not prove what real people do.
  • The interesting outputs are the ones not tuned in: how tricks stack, how fact-checks fade, how counters create randomness, and how friendship networks hold beliefs apart.
  • All 10 benchmark studies were checked online on 5 Nov 2026. Some numbers were only confirmed through summaries; README.md marks which. The decoy effect is fragile in replications (only 11 of 91 attempts in Yang & Lynn 2014).
  • The model treats pushes toward A and B the same way. Real people correct fake negativity more than fake positivity (Muchnik et al. 2013).
  • The headline "all tricks together" number (87%) mostly depends on one guess: how fast pressure saturates (the cap). With the cap halved or raised by half, it ranges from 81% to 91%. Quote it as "roughly 80–90%".
  • The authority trick (new in v2) is not tuned to a real experiment. I couldn't find a clean study measuring "expert says A" on a 50/50 choice. Treat its size as a guess.
  • Code: simulation/sim.py (standard Python, no installs). Assumptions table: simulation/README.md.
