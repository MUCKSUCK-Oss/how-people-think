# FOMO: results (14 Nov 2026)
- **Design and predictions:** saved first in `PREDICTIONS.md`. The research is in `RESEARCH.md`.
- **Data:** **384 decisions, 383 usable** (one answer used the wrong label). 48 AI young workers aged 21–29 in 8 countries (India, the USA, South Korea, Nigeria, Brazil, the UK, Indonesia, Turkey), each seeing all 8 versions. The full numbers are in `output/combined_analysis.txt`.
- **The situation:** put part of your savings (about 3 months' pay) into "NOVA", an invented crypto token up 300% this year that has twice dropped 40% in a week, or keep it in the bank.
- ⚠ **Small groups:** 16 people per risk attitude, so 1 person = 6 points. **Read the results as directions, not exact sizes.**

## The results
| Version | Would invest | Average % of savings | Careful (16) | Feel "behind" (24) | Don't (24) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 62% | 16 | 12% | 75% | 50% |
| + **friend's gains** ("get in before it's too late") | 67% | 16 | **31%** | **88%** | 46% |
| + **influencer** ("LAST CHANCE… don't stay poor 🚀") | **47%** | **10** | 0% | 67% | 26% |
| + crowd ("4 million people bought it") | 69% | 17 | 19% | 79% | 58% |
| + **app** (bonus, confetti, "zero fees ends in 3 days") | **50%** | 13 | 6% | 67% | 33% |
| All four | 60% | 16 | 19% | 79% | 42% |
| All + "**9 in 10** ordinary traders lose money" | 56% | **10** | 12% | 67% | 46% |
| All + 9 in 10 + **"people post wins, not losses"; the friend admits losing more overall** | **29%** | **6** | 0% | 33% | 25% |

## The 5 main findings
1. **Sellers' pressure backfired; only people we know pushed.**
   - The influencer (−16 points) and the app's bonus, confetti and deadline (−12) made people **less** likely to invest.
     - *"That countdown timer and the confetti celebration feel designed to push me into a hasty move."* (a risk-loving AI person, USA)
   - The friend and the crowd pushed only slightly (+4, +6).
   - This is the same pattern as universities, healthcare and targeting: **loud selling triggers suspicion; "people like us" doesn't.**
2. **A friend's gains worked on the careful and on those who feel left behind.**
   - Careful people went from **12% to 31%**, usually with "just 10%, so I'm not left out". This was the only thing that moved them.
   - People told that their college friends were doing better than them went from 75% to **88%**. Those not told that didn't move (50% → 46%).
   - *"Rafael made serious money and it's bugging me, honestly. I'm going with 20% because I can't just keep ignoring this."* (a careful AI person, Brazil)
   - **FOMO here is not greed; it's the fear of being left behind by people you compare yourself with.**
3. **"9 in 10 lose money" barely stopped people; it only made them bet smaller.**
   - Investing fell only from 60% to 56%. The amount fell by a third (16% → 10% of savings).
   - People treated the loss rate as a reason to bet small, not a reason to stay out: *"the 90% failure rate is scary… but I can't completely sit this out or I'll regret it forever."*
   - This matches SEBI: **over 75% of F&O loss-makers kept trading** after two years of losses.
4. **Breaking the friend's story was the strongest protection.**
   - Adding "people post wins, not losses" and the friend admitting their other trades lost more than they gained cut investing to **29%** and the amount to **6%** of savings.
   - Among average-risk people, investing fell from 62% (loss rate) to **12%**.
   - **The fix that works is the one that answers the story, not the one that adds a statistic.** This fits Kaustia & Knüpfer: people hear about neighbours' gains and never their losses.
5. **The tricks don't add up.** All four pressures together (60%) were no higher than base (62%): the seller pressure cancelled out the friend and the crowd.

| 8 | "Silent losers" adds a further drop of ≥10 points | −27 | ✅ |
| 9 | Risk-lovers moved most, careful least, but the careful rise with the friend | Careful moved most with the friend (12 → 31%); risk-lovers were near the ceiling | 🟡 |
| 10 | People feeling "behind" are moved ≥15 points more by the friend | +12 vs −4 (a 16-point gap) | ✅ |
| 11 | AMOUNT follows INVEST; LEFT_OUT highest in friend and all | Roughly (crowd ties all) | 🟡 |

**4 of 11 right, 2 partly, 5 wrong.**
- **What I got wrong:** I treated "invest yes/no" as the main measure. In fact, most AI people make a **small "lottery ticket" bet** even with no pressure, so FOMO shows up in *who* joins (the careful, the left-behind) and *how much*, more than in the overall yes rate. I also expected the friend to be a big push, as in universities and healthcare. Here, the 40%-drop warning in every version kept everyone's bets small.

## Limits
- **48 AI people, one AI model (Claude Haiku),** text only.
- **AI people are probably more careful with money than real 22-year-olds.** SEBI's real numbers (93% of F&O traders losing, 75% continuing) show real people bet far more. These results are directions only.
- **The risk attitude was written into the personas**, so the careful and risk-loving groups were close to 0% and 100%.
- **Every version showed the 40%-drop warning**, which may have made everyone more cautious than a real hype moment.
- **One decision, read once.** Real FOMO builds over weeks of watching friends' posts.
- **Usage:** each batch of 192 decisions took about 40 minutes.
