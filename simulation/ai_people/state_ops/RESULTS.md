# Secret agencies and meme wars: results (14 Nov 2026)
- **Design and predictions:** saved first in `PREDICTIONS.md`. The research is in `RESEARCH.md`.
- **Data:** **336 decisions, all usable**. 48 AI parents in 8 countries where dengue is common (6 per country: 2 trusting, 2 unsure, 2 doubtful), each seeing all 7 feeds. The full numbers are in `output/combined_analysis.txt`.
- **The situation:** a free new dengue vaccine for children ("DengiShield", invented), made abroad, announced by the health ministry. The fake "parent" accounts copy the method of the real Pentagon campaign against China's vaccine in the Philippines (2020–21), but **everything is invented** and no real country is accused.
- ⚠ **Small groups:** 16 people per starting view, so 1 person = 6 points. **Read the results as directions, not exact sizes.**

## % of parents who would vaccinate their child
| Feed | All (48) | Trusting (16) | Unsure (16) | Doubtful (16) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ministry news only | 62% | 100% | 88% | 0% |
| + 3 fake "parent" posts (few likes) | 54% | 100% | 62% | 0% |
| + **12 fake posts, repeating the same lines, hundreds of likes** | **31%** | 94% | **0%** | 0% |
| + 3 fake memes (few likes) | 65% | 100% | 94% | 0% |
| 12 fake posts + **"these accounts were run by a foreign government"** | **75%** | 100% | **100%** | **25%** |
| A warning about fake accounts first, then 12 fake posts | 48% | 100% | 44% | 0% |
| 12 fake posts + **a real local children's doctor replies** | 58% | 100% | 75% | 0% |

**Did the fake posts seem to come from real parents?** "Yes" from **98%** (few posts), **92%** (many posts) and **75%** (memes). After the warning, 29% still said yes.

## The 5 main findings
1. **A fake crowd moved only the undecided middle.**
   - Trusting parents stayed at about 100% and doubtful parents at 0%, whatever they saw.
   - **All the movement came from the unsure parents: from 88% to 0%** with 12 fake posts.
   - This fits the field research in an unexpected way. Real troll campaigns showed little effect on average (Bail 2020, Eady 2023), because most people already have firm views. **The danger is for the people who haven't decided yet**, and a vaccine launch is exactly when many people haven't.
   - *"I'm reading too many parents saying their children got fevers right after this vaccine… why should I risk it when so many people here are warning against it?"* (an unsure parent, India)
2. **The number of posts mattered more than what they said.**
   - 3 fake posts barely moved people (−8 points). 12 posts repeating the same lines with hundreds of likes cut vaccination in half (−31).
   - Nine in ten parents believed the posts came from real local parents. Some even invented details ("I know some of these people").
   - This matches the "repetition makes things feel true" and "everyone agrees" research.
3. **Revealing who was behind the posts undid all of the effect, and then some.**
   - With the platform's notice, vaccination went **higher than with no fake posts at all** (75% vs 62%).
   - **6 of 48 parents who refused with the news alone said yes after learning a foreign government had tried to scare them**, including 4 of the 16 doubtful ones, the only thing that moved them at all.
   - *"A foreign government paid people to pose as Filipino parents and scare us…"* (a doubtful parent, the Philippines). Being manipulated made people angry at the manipulator, not at the vaccine.
4. **A warning in advance helped, but much less than the label** (48% vs 75%).
   - The warning told people what to look for, and most of the unsure still followed the crowd.
   - This matches prebunking research: a real but modest effect (Roozenbeek 2022).
5. **One real doctor won back most, but not all, of what the fake crowd took** (31% → 58%).
   - Several unsure parents said the doctor made sense, but "too many parents here are warning against it". A crowd of strangers outweighed one trusted expert for some people.
   - *"Yes, the doctor has his points, but too many people in our community are warning against it."* (an unsure parent, Nigeria)

**Memes did nothing.** But they had only a few likes, like the 3 text posts, so this mainly shows that **volume and visible agreement** matter more than jokes.

## Predictions check
| # | Prediction | Result | ✓/✗ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | News only: 60–85% vaccinate | 62% | ✅ |
| 2 | A few fake posts: a drop of ≤10 points | −8 | ✅ |
| 3 | Many fakes drop at least 10 points more than a few | −31 vs −8 | ✅ |
| 4 | The effect lands mostly on the already doubtful (≥2× the trusting group's drop) | Doubtful didn't move (already at 0%); the **unsure** moved | ❌ |
| 5 | Memes no stronger than text overall, but stronger on the doubtful | Weaker overall; no effect on the doubtful | 🟡 |
| 6 | The label undoes most of the effect, and may backfire | 75%, above news only (backfire) | ✅ |
| 7 | Prebunking falls between many fakes and the label | 48% (between 31% and 75%) | ✅ |
| 8 | The doctor beats the fake crowd by ≥15 points | +27 | ✅ |
| 9 | Over 50% think the fakes are real; the doubtful most | 75–98%; doubtful highest | ✅ |
| 10 | Gut deciders are moved more than very careful ones | −33 vs −25 (1 person apart) | 🟡 |

**7 of 10 right, 2 partly, 1 wrong.**
- **What I got wrong:** I expected the trolls to work best on people who already agreed with them, as in the Russian troll studies. But in a yes/no choice, the doubtful were already at "no", so there was nothing to move. The undecided were the real target.

## Limits
- **48 AI people, one AI model (Claude Haiku),** text only.
- **The starting views were written into the personas**, which is why the trusting and doubtful groups barely moved. Real people's views are less fixed.
- **One feed, read once.** Real campaigns run for weeks, which could make the effect bigger (repetition) or smaller (people get used to it).
- **The label was very clear and certain.** Real platform labels are often small and unnoticed (Betzer 2025), and companies are rarely certain who runs an account. A real label would probably work less well.
- **AI people sometimes invent details** ("I know some of these people").
- **Usage:** each decision took about 12 seconds; each batch of 168 took about 35 minutes.
