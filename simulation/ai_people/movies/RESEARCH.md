# Movies and propaganda: research before simulating (deep version)
*First version 15 Nov 2026; deepened the same day at Maulik's request ("not enough, do a deeper dive"). Written BEFORE any simulation design.*

**How to read the labels:**
- **[read]:** I read the source text myself (or its full abstract and introduction).
- **[summary]:** from search summaries of papers, reports or news.
- **[CHECK]:** disputed, one-sided or unconfirmed.

**Corrections to the first version:**
- **Pentagon films:** "over 1,800 films and TV shows" was wrong. The authors' own article gives **800+ films and 1,100+ TV titles**.
- ***Fahrenheit 9/11*:** the 2007 study is by **Stroud**.
- ***The Kerala Story*:** its trailer claim was cut from 32,000 women to 3.
- **Top Gun:** all military applications rose only **0.3%** in 1986.

**The question:** can a film or TV show change what people believe or do, even when they know it's "just entertainment"? Who secretly pays for or edits the message? And does telling viewers about it change anything?

**Scope and balance:** this is a global topic. It covers governments of every type (democracies and dictatorships), left- and right-leaning messages, and messages for the government and against it. The point is the **mechanism**, not which side is worse.

## PART A: Who shapes the message (real cases)

### A1. USA: the Pentagon's script deal (the best-documented case)
- **The official rule** [read: Federal Register 2024 proposed rule 2024-15091]:
  - The Defense Department helps a film (jets, ships, bases, soldiers) only if the film:
    - gives a **"reasonably realistic"** picture of the military;
    - either improves public understanding **or may help recruiting and retention**;
    - is "in the best interest of the Nation".
  - The producer must send **a complete script** first.
  - The DoD reviews a rough edit before the cut is locked.
  - Feature films must hold **an official DoD screening in Washington before public release**.
  - The DoD gets about **200 requests a year**.
  - Since 2023–26, producers must also certify that they **didn't change the film to please Chinese censors** (Federal Register 2026-11505) [summary].
- **The scale** [read: Alford & Secker, *The Conversation*, 2017]:
  - The Pentagon has had an entertainment office since **1948**; the CIA set one up in **1996**.
  - From 1911 to 2017, **over 800 feature films** got DoD support, plus **over 1,100 TV titles** (about 900 of them since 2005). The CIA helped **about 60** titles.
  - Their evidence is mostly freedom-of-information documents.
  - **The authors' own limits:**
    - they don't know the effect for most titles;
    - since 2000 the government seems to avoid writing script changes down;
    - they are open critics of the military.
- **Documented script changes** [read: same article; CHECK the original documents]:
  - ***Hulk* (2003):** the lab that creates the monster was made non-military. A capture operation named after a real Vietnam-era chemical weapons programme ("Ranch Hand") was renamed "Angry Man".
  - ***Contact* (1997):** almost all military parts were made civilian, and a scene where the military fears an alien "doomsday machine" was removed.
  - **CIA, *Meet the Parents* (2000):** the CIA said it asked that the character not own CIA torture manuals.
  - **CIA:** it influenced *The Recruit* and *Zero Dark Thirty*; memos suggest threats were played up and government wrongdoing played down.
- **Help refused** [summary]:
  - *Platoon* and *Born on the Fourth of July*: US soldiers commit crimes.
  - *Independence Day*: the military looks helpless.
  - *Windtalkers*: generals look too warlike.
- ***Top Gun: Maverick* (2022)** [summary: the contract was published after a freedom-of-information request]:
  - The Navy could "review the script's thematics" and **add talking points**.
  - It could delete sensitive footage.
  - **Paramount had to show the final cut to make sure it matched "the agreed script".**
  - The Navy said the review was about accuracy and Navy ideals. One person on the production said they knew of no changes requested.
- ***Zero Dark Thirty* (2012)** [summary; CHECK]:
  - A CIA Inspector General report criticised officials for giving the filmmakers secret-level access.
  - The 2014 Senate torture report concluded that harsh interrogation produced **no unique intelligence**, which contradicts the film's suggestion that torture helped find bin Laden.
- **Why it matters:** viewers never see the deal. The film looks independent, but its picture of the military was negotiated. This is the "hidden sender" from the secret-agencies topic (6.8).

### A2. USA: the recruiting myth and the smaller truth
- **"Navy recruiting rose 500% after *Top Gun*" is false** [summary: AAP FactCheck; USNI News 2022].
  - US government data show applications to **all branches rose only 0.3%** in 1986.
  - Navy enlistments rose about **8%** (87,593 → 94,878), but the Navy's ad budget was rising too.
  - **What's real:** recruiters set up desks at some cinemas. In Phoenix, calls about naval aviation doubled, and about 90% of callers had seen the film.
  - **The Navy's own view:** a 1989 spokeswoman said no specific event had changed recruiting, and a **1993 Navy survey** found the film had little effect; brochures worked better.
- ***Captain Marvel* (2019):** Air Force recruiting ads ran before the film in **over 3,600 cinemas**, aimed at women. The Air Force Academy then had its most female applicants in 5 years; the cause is unclear [summary].
- **A small experiment** [read: abstract; Leon-Gil 2024, USF master's thesis]:
  - 125 people saw a military-backed film clip plus a recruiting ad, in different doses.
  - It found **no significant effect** on the overall scales and only a few items moved.
  - It's small, but it's one of the very few direct tests.
- **Lesson:** people believe in the power of film **more** than the evidence supports. That belief is itself a finding.

### A3. Hollywood and China: propaganda by leaving things out
- **PEN America 2020:** studios cut or changed material about Tibet, Taiwan, Hong Kong and LGBTQ characters to get into China, sometimes without being asked.
- **Example:** the Taiwan flag disappeared from the *Top Gun: Maverick* jacket in the 2019 trailer, with a Chinese firm (Tencent) as an early backer. It was back in the 2022 release [summary].
- **The deal:** China lets in about **34 foreign films a year** on a revenue-sharing deal (foreign studios get about 25%) [summary].
- **The US reaction:** since 2023, the Pentagon refuses to help films that bow to Chinese censors. **One government's propaganda rule was made to block another's.**

### A4. China: state films that work as blockbusters
- *Wolf Warrior 2* (2017) and *The Battle at Lake Changjin* (2021) are China's top two films ever (about 5.7 billion yuan each). *Lake Changjin* was state-sponsored and released on the eve of National Day. These are called "main melody" (patriotic) films [summary].
- **The best study in this whole topic: Yao 2024** [read: abstract and conclusion].
  - **Design:** **362 Chinese adults** got a free month on a streaming platform (China's Netflix). They were randomly told to start with:
    - **Wolf Warrior (2015)**, a military-supported action film;
    - a similar **non-political action film**; or
    - **their own choice** between the two.
  - **Results:**
    - **Most people who could choose picked the propaganda film.** It was rated highly and made viewers feel proud.
    - For the roughly 80% who didn't already dislike propaganda, it **raised nationalism, pride in the political system and the belief that the government listens**.
    - **For the roughly 20% who already disliked propaganda, it backfired** and lowered their political support.
    - **Willingness to protest didn't change.**
    - Over the month, **interest faded**: people didn't go on to watch more propaganda.
  - **Why it matters:** this is a real dictatorship, with a full film, random assignment and a pre-registered plan. It shows **both** effects at once: persuasion of the middle and backfire on sceptics.
- **Other China studies:**
  - **Mattingly & Yao 2022** (*Comparative Political Studies*, over 6,800 people): **emotionally stirring** "soft" propaganda videos had **lasting** effects on nationalism [summary].
  - **Zhang & Liu 2025** (working paper): entertainment-style propaganda did **not** raise engagement or attachment, and may put off serious viewers [summary; CHECK].
  - **The evidence is split** on whether entertainment makes propaganda stronger or weaker.

### A5. Russia: money doesn't buy an audience
- The Culture Ministry funds films on "the heroism of Russian warriors" in Ukraine, reportedly about **$395 million** in one round.
- *The Witness* (2023) was backed by the culture and defence ministries and drew little attention. Many such films flopped [summary; CHECK: mostly Western or Ukrainian sources].
- **Contrast with China:** the same strategy is a hit there and a flop here. A film has to work as a film first.

### A6. India: state rewards, censorship and a court-ordered disclaimer (both parties)
- **Under BJP governments (2014–):**
  - *Uri* (2019), *The Kashmir Files* (2022) and *The Kerala Story* (2023) were **made tax-free in several BJP-ruled states**, and the Prime Minister praised *The Kerala Story* in a campaign speech [summary].
  - **The Kerala Story's claim collapsed:**
    - its trailer said 32,000 Kerala women were converted and recruited by ISIS;
    - after criticism, the makers **changed it to the stories of 3 women**;
    - the **Supreme Court** (May 2023) ordered a disclaimer that there is **no authentic data** for the 32,000 figure and that the film is **fictionalised** [summary: Scroll, ThePrint].
    - It's a real-world case of the "fiction label" we can test.
  - **Election Commission 2019:** it **stopped the release of the *PM Narendra Modi* biopic** until the elections ended, calling it "surrogate publicity". The Supreme Court didn't interfere. So **India has a rule that a film can count as election campaigning** [summary].
  - **Academic content analyses** say Muslim characters are increasingly shown as villains or "anti-national" in 2017–20 nationalist films. One older study found 65% of films showed Muslims unfavourably [summary; CHECK: small, mostly qualitative studies].
- **Under Congress governments:**
  - **The Emergency (1975–77):** the satire *Kissa Kursi Ka*, which mocked the government, was banned, and **all its prints were destroyed** (linked to Sanjay Gandhi and minister V.C. Shukla). It was re-shot and released in 1978. The court records differ on the jail sentences [summary; CHECK].
  - **Films Division:** for decades after independence, cinemas had to show **government-made newsreels and documentaries** before films (a licence condition from 1949, rooted in a 1943 wartime rule). The Supreme Court upheld this in 1999 [summary; CHECK when it ended].
- **The balance point:** both main parties have used state power over cinema: one by rewarding friendly films, the other by destroying a hostile one. The habit is shared.
- **Missing:** no study measures these films' effect on votes.

### A7. Pakistan and Turkey
- **Pakistan:** the army's media wing (ISPR) has funded or made dramas and films since the 1990s (*Waar* 2013, *Ehd-e-Wafa* 2019–20). A 2024 Cambridge paper says they are joint productions with private TV and more complex than "simple propaganda"; critics say they glorify the army [summary].
- **Turkey:** *Resurrection: Ertuğrul*, from Turkish state TV, was given free to Pakistan and shown on state TV at **Prime Minister Imran Khan's request**. Its producer is a member of Erdoğan's party. This is propaganda **sent abroad** (soft power). I found reach figures but **no audience study** [summary].

### A8. History: the 20th century
- **The Soviet Union:**
  - Lenin reportedly called cinema the most important art (as recalled by his culture minister). Film was planned, funded and censored by the state.
  - But Soviet audiences preferred Hollywood: *Battleship Potemkin* was pulled from Moscow screens for *The Thief of Bagdad*, which was more popular [summary].
- **Nazi Germany:**
  - Goebbels's ministry controlled film. Only about **96 of 1,097 films (about 9%)** were directly commissioned propaganda; **most were light entertainment**, which Goebbels preferred.
  - The antisemitic *Jud Süß* (1940) was a box-office hit and was shown to SS units [summary]. **Content warning:** hate propaganda, mentioned only as history.
- **The USA in WWII:**
  - The Office of War Information worked with Hollywood. Its director said, in effect, that the easiest way to plant a propaganda idea is through an entertainment film **when people don't realise** they're being propagandised [read: quoted in Yao 2024].
- **The CIA and *Animal Farm* (1954):** the CIA secretly funded the cartoon and had the **ending changed** so the farm animals overthrow the pigs; Orwell's book ends with the pigs still in power [summary].
- **North Korea:**
  - Kim Jong Il wrote *On the Art of the Cinema* (1973).
  - He had South Korean director **Shin Sang-ok and actress Choi Eun-hee kidnapped** (1978) to make better films. They escaped in Vienna in 1986 [summary].
- ***The Birth of a Nation* (1915), the strongest causal study:**
  - Ang 2023 (*American Economic Review*): in the month after the film reached a county, **lynchings were about 4 times as likely**, race riots rose, and Ku Klux Klan support grew later.
  - Kaestner 2023 says the analysis is flawed [summary; CHECK both].

## PART B: Does entertainment actually change people? The evidence

### B1. Strong causal studies (natural experiments and randomised trials)
These are the most trustworthy, because the people who saw the show were chosen by chance or by geography, not by their own taste.

| Study | Show | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **La Ferrara, Chong & Duryea 2012** (*AEJ: Applied*), Brazil | Globo telenovelas showing small families | Globo's TV signal reached different areas in different years (census 1970–91) | **Fertility fell** where Globo arrived, by about as much as 1.6–2 more years of women's education. The effect was strongest for poorer women; a network showing imported shows had no effect [summary] |
| **Jensen & Oster 2009** (*QJE*), India | Cable TV (shows about urban life) | Villages that got cable vs didn't, 2001–03 | **Women's autonomy rose**, acceptance of wife-beating and preference for sons fell, and girls' school enrolment rose [summary; a replication by Iversen & Palmer-Jones exists: CHECK what it found] |
| **Banerjee, La Ferrara & Orozco 2019**, Nigeria | *MTV Shuga* (a drama about HIV) | Randomised trial, about 5,000 young people aged 18–25; control = another TV series | **HIV testing about doubled** at 8 months; fewer STDs among women [summary] |
| **Paluck 2009** (*JPSP*), Rwanda | A reconciliation radio drama | 12 communities randomised; control = a health drama | Changed **what people thought others accepted** (norms) and their behaviour; **barely changed personal beliefs** [summary] |
| **Kim & Patterson 2024** (*APSR*), USA | *The Apprentice* (reality TV) | Uses which channel areas happened to watch | **Raised Trump's vote share in the 2016 Republican primary**, but not other Republicans' and not his general-election result [summary] |
| **Yao 2024**, China | *Wolf Warrior* (military-backed) | Random assignment on a streaming platform, 362 people | See A4: persuaded the middle, backfired on the 20% of sceptics, effect faded [read] |
| **Ang 2023** (*AER*), USA | *The Birth of a Nation* | The film's touring schedule | Lynchings about 4 times as likely in the month after arrival [summary; disputed] |
| **Jacobsen 2011**, USA | *An Inconvenient Truth* | Areas near cinemas showing it | Carbon offsets bought **+50% for 2 months, then back to normal** [summary] |

**What these show:** entertainment *can* change real behaviour (births, HIV tests, votes, even violence). The biggest effects come from **long exposure** (years of telenovelas or cable) or where people had **little other information**. One film's effect usually **fades**.

### B2. Experiments and surveys on single films
| Study | Film | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Hovland, Lumsdaine & Sheffield 1949** | US Army "Why We Fight" films | **Facts rose a lot, opinions a little, motivation to fight barely at all** [summary] |
| **Butler, Koopman & Zimbardo 1995** | *JFK* (1991) | After the film: angrier, more belief in a conspiracy, **less intention to vote** (before vs after, not randomised) [summary] |
| **Schofield & Pavelchak 1989** | *The Day After* (1983, nuclear war, seen by 80–100 million) | More negative views of nuclear weapons, more anti-nuclear intentions. Reagan wrote that it left him "greatly depressed" [summary] |
| **Stroud 2007** (*Political Communication*) | *Fahrenheit 9/11* (2004) | Viewers more negative about Bush even after controls, but the **audience was already about 78% Democrats** (polls): mostly preaching to the choir [summary] |
| **Holbert et al. 2003** | *The West Wing* | Viewers had **more positive images of the presidency**, even of real presidents Bush and Clinton [summary] |
| **Kearns & Young 2017** | *24* (torture) | Torture shown **working raised support** for torture; torture shown **failing didn't lower it** [summary] |
| **Pautz 2015** | *Argo*, *Zero Dark Thirty* | About 25% of 69 students changed their view of the government, mostly for the better [summary] |
| **Schiappa et al. 2005, 2006** | Shows with gay characters | **Less prejudice**, strongest for viewers who knew no gay people (a survey, so it can't prove cause) [summary] |
| **Bridge 2019 vs Romer 2020** | *13 Reasons Why* (suicide) | One study found a rise in boys' suicides after release; a re-analysis says the rise started **before** the show. **Unresolved** [summary] |

### B3. Long-term "cultivation"
- Heavy TV viewers see the world more like TV shows it (Gerbner's "mean world" idea).
- Across five decades of studies, the effect is **small but steady: r ≈ 0.09–0.11** (Morgan 1996; Hermann, Morgan & Shanahan 2021) [summary].
- For the military, this would mean years of heroic films slowly shape civilians' picture of soldiers. **One film can't test this**, which is a limit for our simulation.

### B4. Story persuasion in general
- **Braddock & Dillard 2016** (meta-analysis, 74 studies): stories shift beliefs and attitudes by a small amount (r ≈ 0.17–0.19). One summary says **fiction persuaded better than non-fiction** [summary; CHECK].
- **Green & Brock 2000:** being "carried away" (transported) by a story drives belief change, and **labelling it fact or fiction made little difference** [summary].
- **Appel & Maleckar 2012:** a story persuaded under "fiction", "non-fiction" **and even "fake"** labels [summary].
- **Ebert 2014:** emotions were the same for "true story" and fiction, unless viewers got a break to think about it being fictional [summary].

## PART C: Does telling people about it help? (disclosure)
This is the key question for our simulation, because nobody has tested **"the military approved this script"** on viewers.

- **The persuasion knowledge model** (Friestad & Wright 1994): once people spot that something is trying to persuade them, they **reread the whole message** as a sales pitch (the "change of meaning") [summary].
- **The advertising evidence** (Eisend et al. 2020, *Journal of Advertising*, a meta-analysis of about 60 papers): telling people content is **sponsored** makes them **recognise the persuasion and resist more**, and makes them **like the brand and trust the source less**. But the effect depends on wording, timing and whether people notice the notice [summary].
- **State-media labels:** a YouTube label saying RT is funded by the Russian government **cancelled** the opinion shift from RT's misinformation (Harvard Misinformation Review) [summary]. This matches our secret-agencies result, where naming the foreign sender undid the fake crowd and even backfired.
- **"Our side's propaganda" is judged differently:** Dunk et al. 2026 (*European Journal of Social Psychology*, 282 Canadians). When asked openly, people rated a video **from their own government as *more* propaganda-like** than the same video from a foreign government. With hidden measures, the difference disappeared [summary; CHECK].
- **Backfire on sceptics** (Yao 2024): even without a label, people who already dislike propaganda reacted against the film. A disclosure might **create** more such sceptics.
- **The gap:** no study adds a "made with defence-ministry approval" notice to an entertainment film and measures enjoyment, belief and recruiting interest. **This is what our simulation can add.**

## PART D: Ten lessons (plain words)
1. **Who pays is hidden.** The Pentagon (800+ films), the CIA, China's market, India's tax breaks, Pakistan's army and Turkish state TV all shape films that viewers think are independent.
2. **Propaganda often works by leaving things out:** removing military crimes (*Hulk*, *Contact*), a flag (*Top Gun*), or a whole film (*Kissa Kursi Ka*).
3. **One film rarely changes action;** years of exposure can (telenovelas and birth rates, cable TV and women's status).
4. **Facts and feelings move first, behaviour last** (Hovland 1949 → *An Inconvenient Truth* 2006).
5. **Persuade the middle, lose the sceptics** (Yao 2024). This matches our secret-agencies result, where only the unsure moved.
6. **"It's fiction" doesn't protect people** (Green & Brock; Appel). But a court made *The Kerala Story* add exactly that disclaimer.
7. **Showing something work teaches more than showing it fail** (*24*, *Zero Dark Thirty*).
8. **Film works best where people have no real-life experience** (gay characters, soldiers for civilians, urban life for rural India).
9. **Money isn't enough:** China's films are hits and Russia's flop; Soviet audiences preferred Hollywood; Goebbels preferred entertainment over open propaganda.
10. **People overrate film power:** the "500% Top Gun" myth, and the Navy's own 1993 survey found brochures worked better.

## PART E: Ideas for the simulation (Maulik decides)
- **Basic set-up:**
  - Young people aged 17–24 in several countries (with and without conscription, democracies and others).
  - Starting views of their own military: proud, neutral or critical.
  - They read a **detailed scene-by-scene summary** of an invented film (no real film or country named).
- **Possible versions:**
  1. **No film** (baseline).
  2. **A heroic "no soldier left behind" film** (Maulik's idea).
  3. **A critical film** (soldiers commit and cover up abuse, like *Platoon*).
  4. **The heroic film + a disclosure** before it: "made with the defence ministry's help; the ministry approved the script and the final cut." **This is the new test.**
  5. **The heroic film + a disclosure of what was cut:** "the ministry removed a scene showing a civilian death." This tests propaganda by leaving things out.
  6. **The heroic film + a fiction disclaimer** ("fictionalised; events invented"), like the court-ordered *Kerala Story* line.
  7. **A "breaking rules works" film:** the hero tortures a suspect and saves the city (*24*).
- **Answers:**
  - interest in joining the military (1–7);
  - trust in the military (1–7);
  - "Did the film feel like propaganda?" (yes/no);
  - support for harsh interrogation (for version 7);
  - a reason.
- **Predictions to write before data:**
  - the heroic film moves the neutral group most;
  - critical people backfire (Yao);
  - the disclosure cuts trust gains but not enjoyment;
  - the fiction disclaimer does little (Green & Brock);
  - the "cut scene" disclosure hurts more than the general approval disclosure.
- **Biggest limits (state them in the paper):**
  - AI people read a summary, not 2 hours of music and faces, so effects are probably **smaller** than a real film's.
  - One exposure can't test cultivation.
  - AI models may be **more suspicious of militaries** than real 18-year-olds.

## PART F: Spy agencies and film around the world (balanced)
*Added 16 Nov 2026 (overnight run). Same labels as above: [read] / [summary] / [CHECK]. Every country is judged by the same test: **does the agency or ministry shape the story, and does the audience know?** Parts A1–A8 already cover the Pentagon deal, China's quota, Russia's flops, India's tax breaks, ISPR dramas and Ertuğrul; this part adds the **intelligence-agency** side and closes gaps. Video evidence is in `sources/topic_notes/youtube_night_2026-11-15.md`.*

### F1. USA: CIA and Pentagon
- **Pentagon:** a written script-approval deal (A1). The liaison Phil Strub called changes "a process of accommodation" and recruiting help "a byproduct" (2012 radio interview, quoted by Tom Secker) [read: transcript].
- **A small example with documents:** on *Iron Man* (2008), Strub objected to one line he felt made light of suicide; director Jon Favreau angrily offered "walk over hot coals" and Strub agreed. Neither line stayed in [summary: Strub's own account in *MCU: The Reign of Marvel Studios*, via Looper and The Direct].
- **CIA:** it has had an entertainment liaison since the mid-1990s (Chase Brandon was the first). A 2012 CIA inspector-general audit found *Zero Dark Thirty* got "significantly more support" than seven other projects; there were at least three internal inquiries, and the CIA overhauled its Hollywood rules afterwards [summary: Yahoo/AP, POGO].
- **Hiding the role:** Secker documents that Strub played down Pentagon help on *Tomorrow Never Dies* and *The Avengers* even where credits or documents show it [read: transcript; CHECK the Bond "Vietnam joke" detail, which I could not confirm].
- **Same standard check:** the USA is the best-documented case **because** its records can be requested by law (FOIA). Being documented is not the same as doing the most.

### F2. Russia / USSR: the KGB's TV hero and the FSB's film prize
- ***Seventeen Moments of Spring* (1973):** a 12-part TV series about a Soviet spy inside Nazi Berlin. KGB chief **Yuri Andropov** backed it to improve the KGB's image; the "chief consultant" in the credits under a false name was **Semyon Tsvigun, deputy head of the KGB** [summary: New Lines Magazine; a CIA Studies in Intelligence article; IMDb credits]. It was hugely popular, and its hero Stierlitz is still a household name [CHECK: viewing figures].
- **The FSB's arts prize:** since **2006** the FSB has given yearly awards for films, TV, books and music that show its officers in a good light. This revives a KGB prize that ran from 1978 to 1988. One official said the aim was a "positive image" that does not "discredit" security employees [summary: CEPA; Foreign Policy "The KGB Oscars"].
- One winning film showed an exiled oligarch as the mastermind of a terror attack. Foreign Policy, citing journalist Andrei Soldatov, reported the FSB spent about **$7 million** on producing *Personal Number* (2004) [summary; CHECK].
- **War films since 2022:** *The Witness* (2023, about the war in Ukraine) opened in 1,131 cinemas and took about $70,000 against a budget of about $2M. *Crimean Bridge* cost about $3M and earned $710,000 [summary: Al Jazeera, The Insider, The Guardian via inkl]. **Lesson:** an agency can pay for the film, but not for the audience (same as A5).

### F3. China: the MSS's first film, and PLA blockbusters
- **The Ministry of State Security (MSS) backed its first feature film in 2026:** ***Scare Out*** (directed by Zhang Yimou, released on Chinese New Year, 17 Feb 2026). The MSS said it should "strengthen the people's defence line" and serve "national security education". Zhang said security officers accompanied the production throughout [summary: AP via The Star and Washington Times; Semafor].
- **TV:** the CCTV spy drama *The Secret Path* (Apr 2026) was made "under the guidance" of the MSS; *Spy Game* (2023) was about a foreign spy network run through an online game [summary: SCMP; AP].
- **Purpose (mechanism):** these productions encourage ordinary people to **report suspicious behaviour**. The MSS also posts almost daily on social media about real cases [summary: AP]. This is propaganda that recruits viewers as watchers, not just admirers.
- **The military:** *Operation Red Sea* (2018) was the **first official cooperation of the PLA Navy** with a major film, based loosely on the 2015 evacuation from Yemen [summary: USNI Proceedings; The Diplomat]. For *Wolf Warrior 2* I could not confirm specific PLA help tonight [CHECK].
- **Effect evidence:** the best experiment in this whole topic is Chinese (Yao 2024, A4). A military-supported film raised nationalism in most viewers and backfired on sceptics.

### F4. India: RAW on screen, under both parties
- **Spy films:** *Raazi* (2018) adapts Harinder Sikka's novel *Calling Sehmat* about a woman who spied for RAW in Pakistan before 1971. Sikka says the story came from the agent's son; the rest is fictionalised, and **no official source has confirmed** it [summary: Wikipedia, The Quint]. YRF's *Tiger*/*Pathaan* films show RAW and ISI agents working together and falling in love, which right-leaning commentators now mock as "fake secular" [read: Abhijit Chavda transcript].
- ***Dhurandhar* (2025) and *Dhurandhar 2* (2026):** a fictional RAW-linked agent in Karachi, "inspired by real events" (26/11, Kandahar, Pulwama-era failures). Huge hit; Ladakh made it tax-free. The opposition called the sequel "paid propaganda", the BJP defended it, and critics flagged its use of archival footage of the PM [summary: Bollywood Hungama; NewKerala/IANS]. **I found no evidence of government funding.** The debate is about blending fact and fiction (see the YouTube notes, section 5).
- **Official gatekeeping (BJP era):** in **July 2020** the Defence Ministry asked the censor board (CBFC) to tell producers to get a **no-objection certificate** before showing films or web series about the Army. Industry sources said an NOC was already usual for Army-themed films. Legal commentators called the wording "broad, vague and subjective" [summary: Tribune, LiveLaw, The Wire].
- **Congress-era equivalents** (A6): *Kissa Kursi Ka* prints destroyed (Emergency, 1975–77) and compulsory government newsreels. A viral 2026 clip shows the 1980 film *Qurbani* paying tribute to Indira and Sanjay Gandhi [summary: Free Press Journal; CHECK context].
- **Same standard:** RAW itself is not known to fund films (unlike the MSS or FSB). India's levers are **tax breaks, censor certificates, ministry NOCs and praise from leaders**, used by both main parties.

### F5. Pakistan: ISPR (the army's media wing), less so the ISI
- ISPR has made or backed dramas and films for decades; *Shahpar* (an air-force drama on ARY) was ISPR-funded, and *Sher Dil* (2019) is listed as an ISPR project made with a private channel [summary: ISPR media productions list; Tribune].
- **Funding is often unclear or denied:** the director of *Waar* (2013) denies the persistent rumour that ISPR paid for it. *Sher Dil* is also described as privately funded with air-force logistics [summary: Tribune; The Peninsula].
- A 2024 Cambridge paper calls these co-productions more complex than "simple propaganda" (A7).
- **ISI-specific productions:** I found **no reliable source** showing the ISI (rather than ISPR) funds dramas [CHECK].
- **Same standard:** like India's NOC, the line between "help with tanks" and "control of the story" is invisible to viewers.

### F6. Israel: *Fauda* and Mossad
- ***Fauda*** (2015–) was created by **Lior Raz** (who served in the undercover unit Duvdevan) and journalist **Avi Issacharoff**. Critics, including the BDS movement and Hamas, call it propaganda that softens the occupation; the creators say it gives Palestinian characters real motives and shows the cost of war [summary: Algemeiner, Times of Israel, Forward].
- **I found no evidence that Mossad or the army formally cooperates** on *Fauda* [CHECK]. The influence here is **who makes it**: ex-soldiers telling the story from inside the unit, which builds credibility.
- **Same standard:** this is like *Dhurandhar* or *Top Gun*. No state money, but the national-security viewpoint is the only one inside the camera.

### F7. UK: MI6, the MoD and James Bond
- The **Ministry of Defence** has long supported Bond films. Producer Barbara Broccoli called the MoD "very supportive". *No Time to Die* (2021) filmed on HMS Dragon and at RAF Brize Norton; the MoD called this part of normal **commercial** arrangements [summary: Forces News; RAF news].
- **Daniel Craig was made an honorary Royal Navy Commander** (Bond's rank) just before the premiere [summary: Yahoo/Fox].
- **MI6 and scripts:** I found no reliable source showing MI6 shapes Bond scripts. One critical piece (RT, Russian state media) claims MoD/MI6/CIA ties led to rewrites; **RT is itself a state propaganda outlet**, so this is not usable evidence [summary; CHECK].
- **Same standard:** the honorary rank is a clear case of state and film promoting each other.

### F8. Turkey: MIT and state TV series
- ***Teşkilat*** (TRT state TV, 2021–) follows agents of the intelligence agency **MIT**. Some scenes were reportedly shot **inside MIT's Ankara campus**; Nordic Monitor (a critical outlet) says MIT sponsors it. Its main villain resembles Mohammed Dahlan, a real exile whom Turkey's government accuses of plotting against it [summary: Middle East Eye; Atalayar; New Lines Magazine]. A Turkish columnist called it "outdated propaganda" [summary].
- *Ertuğrul* (TRT) was given free to Pakistan for state TV (A7).
- **Caveat:** most sources on MIT's role are hostile to the government; I found no official confirmation [CHECK].

### F9. North Korea: the extreme case
- Kim Jong Il ran the film studio from the late 1960s to strengthen his father's rule, wrote *On the Art of the Cinema* (1973), and had director **Shin Sang-ok and actress Choi Eun-hee kidnapped** in 1978 to improve the films. They escaped in Vienna in 1986 [read: Johnny Harris transcript; summary: Wikipedia, Dazed].
- Films were screened in factories, farms and army units [read: Harris; CHECK].
- His official birth story (sacred Mt Paektu, 1942) contradicts Soviet records (1941, in the USSR). A film-like myth was built into the state's own biography [summary].

### F10. Qatar: a different model, credibility instead of control
- Qatar funds **Al Jazeera** (from 1996, about 500M riyals ≈ $137M at the start). PolyMatter argues it is broadly credible on everything **except Qatar itself** [read: PolyMatter transcript; summary: EBSCO, Cambridge Clarion].
- **Why it matters for Part F:** the cheapest and most lasting influence may be a *trusted* outlet with one blind spot, not a hero film.

### F11. How film propaganda works: the mechanisms, with evidence
| Mechanism | What it looks like | Evidence (strength) |
|---|---|---|
| **Hero framing** | The agency or army is brave, competent and rule-bound | Yao 2024 (strong RCT, China, A4): raised nationalism in ~80%, backfired in ~20% |
| **Leaving things out** | Scenes cut (civilian deaths, suicide jokes, losing Vietnam) | Pentagon records and Strub's own *Iron Man* story (documents, strong); our sim: the "cut scene" notice cut trust by 0.44 (AI people, weak) |
| **Enemy images** | One group is always the villain; dialogue cues (e.g. religious slogans) | Content analyses of Muslim villains in Indian films (A6, weak/qualitative); *Birth of a Nation* and lynchings (Ang 2023, strong but disputed) |
| **Blurring fact and fiction** | "Inspired by real events"; real footage mixed with invented scenes | *The Kerala Story* court-ordered disclaimer (A6); *Dhurandhar* (F4); Appel & Maleckar: fiction labels don't stop belief (B4) |
| **Music and emotion** | Songs, swelling scores, crowd scenes | Mattingly & Yao 2022: emotional "soft" propaganda had lasting effects (A4, medium) [CHECK: no study isolating music alone] |
| **Repetition across many products** | Same message in 800+ films and 1,100+ TV titles | Pentagon count (A1); cultivation research shows small long-run effects (B3) |
| **Hidden sponsorship** | Credits small or absent; liaisons deny help | Strub's denials (F1); Eisend 2020: disclosure lowers persuasion (C); our sim: the approval notice cancelled the trust gain |
| **Rewards and gatekeeping** | Tax-free status, NOCs, honorary ranks, FSB prizes | F2, F4, F7 (documented; effect on audiences untested) |
| **Turning viewers into watchers** | Films that urge reporting "suspicious" people | MSS *Scare Out* (F3) (new; no study yet) |
| **Credibility laundering** | A trusted outlet that is silent only on its owner | Al Jazeera/Qatar (F10) (argument; no causal study) |

### F12. What this adds (plain words)
1. **Every country studied does this.** The tools differ: the USA trades equipment for scripts; Russia and China pay for and award films; India and Pakistan use tax breaks, certificates and military "help"; Turkey uses state TV; North Korea owns everything; Qatar buys credibility.
2. **Intelligence agencies (CIA, KGB/FSB, MSS, MIT) mostly want a better *image*, while armies want *recruits*.** China's MSS adds a third goal: getting citizens to report each other.
3. **Hiding the sponsor is the common thread.** It is the one thing a viewer can't see, and the one thing our simulation found matters most when revealed.
4. **Money does not guarantee an audience** (Russia), but a good film with quiet help can (China's blockbusters, *Top Gun*, *Dhurandhar*).
5. **The biggest gap remains:** almost no study measures whether these spy films change real viewers' trust in their intelligence agencies. Only Yao 2024 comes close.
